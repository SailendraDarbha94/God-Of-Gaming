# 01. Scope

## Goal

By the end of iteration 2 the founder can sign a Bengaluru cafe on during one visit, from a phone, and the cafe's counter can keep the app's availability true from then on. A cafe goes live in two steps. First it is listed: players see it on the website and in the app, and book by phone or WhatsApp. Then it takes app bookings on the stations the owner pledges, once a test booking has passed at its own counter and the founder has approved it; the database enforces that order (ADR 0016).

Payments stay out. The evidence does not show that cafes refuse to list without them: Hash, GoPlaya, GameSpotter, GameBiller and Olympus all leave the money with the cafe, and Console lists cafes for free (partner research of 16 Nov 2026, from `india_competitors.md` and `owner_player_voice.md` §2 in `research_notes/Gaming cafe booking aggregator research/`). What remains is the no-show risk, so this iteration records no-shows to decide deposits on data (open decision 8).

| Player                      | `apps/player`                                                  | Never be sold a seat the cafe cannot honour; see the cafe's notice and grace periods; show a booking code at the desk; learn why the cafe cancelled                                                                                                                                             |

## Evidence that shaped the scope

- Partner tools elsewhere share a core that has nothing to do with money: today's and upcoming bookings, opening and blocking inventory, editing prices, cancelling, and staff roles. Gaming tools add a live floor view by station and walk-in entry in one or two clicks; Hash says it books a walk-in "in two clicks" ([Hash](https://www.hashforgamers.com/gaming-cafe-software), a company claim, read 16 Nov 2026). Payouts and invoices exist only where the platform collects the player's money. This comparison of Playo, Hudle, KheloMore, District, Hash, Olympus, Console and GameBiller comes from the partner research of 16 Nov 2026, built on `playo_product.md`, `india_competitors.md` and the companies' own pages.
- One person usually runs the counter, taking cash, running stations and cleaning (`owner_player_voice.md`, Owners §1). App notifications must be hard to miss.
- Bookings that venues did not honour are a large share of low-star reviews of Indian booking apps (`playo_sentiment.md` §1; ADR 0016).
- No source gives the share of walk-ins against advance bookings, turn-away rates or no-show rates in Indian cafes (`red_team.json`, `kill_criteria`: "none exists"), and no Indian owner statement says what would make them sign (`owner_player_voice.md`, Gaps). The pilot has to measure these, which is why check-ins and no-shows are recorded.
- "Cafe counters have a PC" is an assumption from the README; console-only parlours may not have one. The board therefore works on a phone, and the visit records which device the counter uses.

## In scope

- `apps/partner`: phone OTP sign-in, a cafe switcher, the counter board, the listing and stations editors, hours and closures, app-booking settings, the team, the preview with the partner terms, and operator pages (`05-partner-app.md`).
- Database: the reference data (cities, areas, games) as a migration; platform operators, invitations, partner terms acceptance, closures, an audit log, counter blocks and the counter lifecycle, the partner read functions, the operator's approval and suspension of cafes, account deletion (M7), and the changes to `create_booking`, `get_availability`, `cancel_booking`, `confirm_booking` and the bookings select policy (`03-database.md`).
- Packages: partner entities and rules in `@gog/domain`; four partner repositories with Supabase and fixture implementations and shared contract suites in `@gog/data`; a browser session client; a partner ESLint config (`04-packages.md`).
- The hazards found in iteration 1, closed before the first membership exists: "My bookings" leaking other players' bookings to a cafe member, the bookings policy showing a member every booking at their cafe with the booker's user id, and `cancel_booking` letting any member cancel any of them. A fourth is a trap rather than a leak: any member-read policy on a catalog table would show that member's drafts in their own player app, so none is added (ADR 0015).
- Changes to the player app and the website, listed below.
- Player launch readiness (M7): account deletion in the app, a web page for deletion requests, and the final player privacy notice, which the stores and ADR 0011 require before public app bookings (L5).
- The launch path in `07-launch.md`, including the Send SMS hook: its vendor-independent part is built in M1, so staging can sign people in with private test numbers, and it can only be finished once the founder has a DLT-registered SMS vendor.

## Changes to existing apps and packages

- `apps/player`: "My bookings" lists only the caller's own bookings (data layer, M0). Cancel disappears at the start: `isCancellable` compares `startsAt` instead of `endsAt`, because `cancel_booking` now refuses a booking from its start (M1). A cafe that does not take app bookings reads "Not taking app bookings" with the cafe's WhatsApp link where there is one (M2). The day picker and slot grid respect the cafe's notice period, and the seat stepper stops at the tier's pledged stations (M2). Every booking shows its booking code (M3). A booking the cafe cancelled shows the cafe's reason, and the booking screen states the cafe's grace period (M5). The profile gains Delete account, and sign-in shows the consent wording (M7). The phone helpers and `hasLapsed` / `effectiveStatus` move into `@gog/domain` (M1).
- `apps/web`: a cafe that does not take app bookings reads as listed, with how to book by phone or WhatsApp (M2). The privacy page says what a cafe receives and that the platform may contact a player about a booking (M3, ADR 0017), and becomes the final player privacy notice in M7. The for-cafes page describes the counter board in place of "Counter tool later" and may link to the partner app (M5). A static page explains how to delete an account in the app or, without the app, by a mailto or WhatsApp link to the founder (M7), which Google Play asks for. No form, no sign-in (ADR 0004).
- `packages/domain`, `packages/data`, `packages/eslint-config`, `packages/tsconfig`, CI and the root tooling: `04-packages.md` and `06-verification.md`.

## Milestones

Each milestone is a thin vertical slice through the database, the packages and the apps, and ends with checks that a reviewer can run. The parts of a milestone can be built in parallel against these documents. M0 to M5 are the build; M6 is a stretch to cut first if time is short; M7 is required before L5, not a stretch, and can be built alongside M3 to M5; L depends on the founder's accounts. With iteration 1's process (parallel builders, then integration and review), a plausible pace is M0 to M2 in the first week, M3 and M4 in the second, and M5 and M7 with the final integration in the third.

Each acceptance check says where it runs, because fixtures cannot hold state shared by two apps: each browser tab has its own fixture world, the fixture catalog never sees a world's edits (`04-packages.md`), and the player app is a separate runtime. **Fixtures** means the partner app alone, in Playwright and by hand at 360 px and on a 1366 by 768 screen. **CI's stack** means pgTAP and the contract suites against `supabase start`, which is where behaviour across personas is proven. **Vitest** means the player's and the website's own tests over domain objects, which prove how they render. What needs both apps on one real backend (the alert reaching a counter, the code matching across apps, the player seeing the cafe's reason) is checked by hand in the staging rehearsal before L4 (`06-verification.md`), not in a milestone.

- The player app's first run: iteration 1 never ran `apps/player` on a simulator or a device, and every later milestone changes it, so M0 builds it for the iOS Simulator on this Mac (`pnpm --filter @gog/player ios`, a local development build that needs no Expo account) against fixtures, and fixes whatever stops it starting. - **Acceptance:** CI is green with the partner build, and `pnpm --filter @gog/partner typecheck` passes with no environment variables set. By hand on the iOS Simulator against fixtures: the player app signs in with any mobile number and code 123456, opens a cafe, holds and confirms a squad, and shows it under My bookings. - Packages: partner entities, roles and `PartnerError`; the phone helpers moved; `createBrowserSessionClient`; `@gog/data/partner` with `PartnerRepository`; the fixture world; the first partner contract cases; the catalog contract suite run as a signed-in member whose memberships its harness checks first. The fixture booking repository refuses a cancellation from the start.
- Apps: sign-in by phone and code; invite acceptance after sign-in, on every start with a stored session and before each read of "My cafes"; the cafe switcher with "Check for invitations"; a read-only cafe overview; sign-out. The player's Cancel stops at the start.
- Launch tooling: the vendor-independent Send SMS hook with its Deno tests in CI (`07-launch.md`), so staging can sign people in with private test numbers before DLT.
- **Acceptance:** on CI's stack, through the partner contract suite, and on fixtures: the owner persona sees Pixel Den and Level Up Lounge as owner (Level Up as a draft), the staff persona sees Pixel Den as staff, the stranger persona sees no cafe, and the operator persona sees all seven as operator. - Packages: input schemas and validators, `publishChecklist`, `previewCafe`, the new public fields of `Cafe` and `StationTier`, `isBookable` and `effectiveLeadMinutes`; `ListingRepository` (M2 methods) and `MembershipRepository`; the fixture booking repository applies the same rules as SQL.
- Apps: the operator's new-cafe form, the listing editor (details, hours, games), the stations and tiers editor, the team page, the preview with the publish checklist, terms acceptance and publishing. The player and website changes for listed-only cafes and the notice period.
- **Acceptance:** On fixtures at 360 px (Playwright's operator spec and by hand): the operator creates a draft cafe with a tier, stations and weekly hours and invites the owner persona's number; the owner persona signs in, joins, opens the preview and accepts the terms; the operator publishes, and the partner app shows the cafe as Listed with app bookings Off. On CI's stack: pgTAP 008 (anon sees the published cafe with `app_bookings_enabled` false; a draft without an owner's acceptance is `GG014` naming `owner_terms`; an owner cannot demote or remove another owner; an expired invite does not block a new one) and the M2 contract cases (the stranger's `createHold` at a cafe not taking app bookings is `cafe_not_bookable`; a persona signed in before being invited joins through `acceptInvites`). Vitest: the player app and the website render a cafe with app bookings off as "Not taking app bookings" with its WhatsApp link, and the player's slot grid drops starts inside the notice period.

## Founder prerequisites

Agents cannot create accounts. `07-launch.md` lists each one with its cost, lead time and the milestone or launch step it blocks. In short: none of M0 to M7 needs an account, because CI and the fixtures cover the build, and each acceptance check above runs on fixtures, on CI's stack or in Vitest. A business identity comes first, because DLT, the partner terms and organisation store accounts all need one. A real-phone trial against a hosted stack (the staging rehearsal) needs the staging project, M1's Send SMS hook running there with private test numbers, and an Expo account for the player build on the test phone. Listing a real cafe needs the production Supabase project, Vercel Pro, the domain, DLT registration with an SMS vendor (any real owner's sign-in depends on it), reviewed partner terms, and the founder's own operator row, inserted after the founder's first sign-in there. Taking app bookings from the public needs M7 and the player app in the stores as well; on a personal Google Play account that includes a closed test of at least 14 days.
