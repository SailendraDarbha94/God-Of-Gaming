# 07. Launch in Bengaluru

Going live happens in two steps, as `01-scope.md` describes. **Listed**: the website and the partner app run on production hosting with a production Supabase project and real SMS sign-in, and the first cafes appear as listed. Players see them and book by phone or WhatsApp. **Bookable**: the player app is in the stores, and each cafe takes app bookings once a test booking has passed at its counter and the founder has approved it (`operator_approve_app_bookings`). Nothing here is built by agents alone: every step below needs one of the founder's accounts.

Prices and lead times are as the platform research read them on 16 November 2026; "undated" means the page shows no date. Recheck them before paying.

## Order

- **L0, now, in parallel with the build**: settle the business identity first (0), which DLT, the partner terms and organisation store accounts all need; decide the name (1), so DLT (2) can be filed with the brand in the templates; open the SMS vendor account (3); create staging (4) with the Auth settings below, and once M1's Send SMS hook runs there in its no-send mode, sign the founder in with a private test number and insert that number's operator row (10); create the Expo account (4a); brief counsel (8). For a personal Google Play account (open decision 17): once open decision 14 fixes the package name, create the Play account and start a closed test with at least 12 testers on the M2 player build, so production access can be requested when M5 is accepted.
- **L1, production stack** (after M2 merges; needs 1, 5, 6, 7): the Supabase project, its custom domain and the two Vercel projects, as below.
- **L2, SMS** (needs 2, 3 and L1): the vendor call added to the Send SMS hook, tested end to end on staging with the founder's phone, then switched on in production; then the founder's first sign-in on production and the operator row (production step 4).
- **L3, the first cafe listed** (needs L1, L2 and counsel's partner terms, with M2 deployed): an onboarding visit that stops after publishing. The cafe is listed only.
- **L4, the counter tools** (M3 to M5 deployed; needs 4a): a full rehearsal on staging (`06-verification.md`), the first time both apps run against one real backend; then, per cafe, a visit or a return visit that ends with the test booking, the founder's approval and app bookings switched on for a small pledge.
- **L5, the player app public** (needs 9, M7 merged and deployed, and counsel's player privacy notice and consent wording): store builds from EAS, the store links set on the website, and app bookings on at every cafe that passed its test booking.
- **L6, the first weekend**: the founder keeps `/ops` open on an always-on device through the opening hours of bookable cafes, rings the squad of any cafe cancellation it lists, phones each cafe after its first app bookings, and revisits after seven days to adjust pledges, notice and prices.

## The production Supabase project

1. Create it in Mumbai, chosen explicitly: `ap-south-1` is a "specific region", and the general Asia-Pacific choice is Singapore ([regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). Pro plan, spend cap on.
2. Apply the migrations with the pinned CLI (`npx supabase@2.116.0 link --project-ref <ref>` then `db push`). Never apply `seed.sql`, and never run `supabase config push` from this repository: `config.toml` carries placeholder Twilio settings and the test numbers, as its comments warn. The cities, areas and games come from migration `20261117115900_reference_data.sql`, not from the seed: check that `select count(*) from public.areas` is 18 and `select count(*) from public.games` is 16, and, after the partner app's first deployment, that `/ops/new-cafe` lists the Bengaluru areas and the Games picker is filled.
3. Configure Auth in the dashboard instead, on staging too (`supabase/config.toml` sets `enable_confirmations = true` for local stacks only, and is never pushed):
   - Phone sign-in through the Send SMS hook (L2), not a built-in provider.
   - Phone confirmations on; the Email provider off; anonymous sign-ins off. With confirmations off, GoTrue returns a session from a phone sign-up without any code and applies `updateUser({ phone })` to an unused number at once (`supabase/auth` master, `internal/api/signup.go` and `user.go`, read 16 Nov 2026), so anyone could take a number they do not hold. On staging, check that `signUp({ phone, password })` returns no session and that `updateUser({ phone })` leaves `auth.users.phone` unchanged until the code is entered (`06-verification.md`). The documentation offers no switch for phone-and-password sign-up ([passwords](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/passwords), read by the review on 16 Nov 2026), which is why every partner check also requires a session opened with an SMS code (ADR 0014).
   - No test phone numbers on production, and never the repository's numbers or codes, which are public. Staging, until L2, has private test numbers set in its dashboard (below).
   - Raise the SMS rate limit before launch: the default is 30 SMS an hour for the whole project ([rate limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits)). Whether the limit applies when the hook sends is unverified; check it on staging. The per-IP limit on sign-ins (30 per five minutes) is shared by everyone on one cafe's Wi-Fi.
   - CAPTCHA stays off: it is project-wide and would make the player app send a token too ([CAPTCHA](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-captcha)).
   - Optional: a Before User Created hook that refuses numbers outside +91 (available on Free and Pro, per [auth hooks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks)).
4. In L2, once the founder has signed in on production by SMS: check that `select count(*), min(created_at), bool_and(phone_confirmed_at is not null) from auth.users where phone = '91<ten digits>'` returns one confirmed row created at that first sign-in. A row created earlier means someone registered the number first (GoTrue lets anyone do that with a password, ADR 0014): delete that user in the dashboard and sign in again. Then insert the founder's operator row in the SQL editor: `insert into public.platform_operators (phone) values ('91<ten digits>');`. Nothing in git holds it (ADR 0014). Do the same on staging.
5. Check that pg_cron lists `expire-holds` and `settle-finished-bookings`, and that the `cafe-images` bucket exists.
6. Activate `api.<domain>` (`supabase domains create`, then `reverify` and `activate`; the documentation now shows CLI v3's `--remote` flag, so check the v2 syntax with `--help`). Point both web apps and the player at it. Both apps pin their auth storage key, so the switch signs nobody out (`04-packages.md`). The Indian ISP block of `supabase.co` in February and March 2026 was lifted on 3 March after the order was rescinded ([Supabase blog](https://supabase.com/blog/navigating-regional-network-blocks), 2026-03-26), and no recurrence was found; one user reported that a custom domain "didn't fix it for everyone" ([issue 43142](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/issues/43142)). The domain is resilience, not a guarantee.

## The Send SMS hook (M1, then L2)

ADR 0003 already decides that production OTP goes through a DLT-registered vendor via Supabase's Send SMS hook. A hosted project needs an SMS provider even for test numbers, to which it sends nothing ([testing auth flows](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/testing-auth-flows), read by the review on 16 Nov 2026), so the work is split and staging can sign people in before DLT is done:

- M1, no account needed: an Edge Function, `supabase/functions/send-sms/`, registered as an HTTP Send SMS hook. It verifies the Standard Webhooks signature (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`, with a secret of the form `v1,whsec_…`), reads the phone and `sms.otp` from the payload (at most 20 KB), and answers an empty 200 on success, all within the hook's 5 seconds including retries ([Send SMS hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/send-sms-hook)). With no vendor configured it sends nothing, logs neither the number nor the code, and answers 200. The signature check, the payload parsing and the responses are a pure module with Deno unit tests, which CI runs (`06-verification.md`); CI excludes `edge-runtime` from `supabase start`, so the function itself is checked by hand on staging.
- Staging, from M1: deploy the function in its no-send mode and register it as staging's Send SMS hook; add private test numbers with their codes, and "Test OTPs Valid Until", in staging's dashboard, never in git and never the repository's numbers. Those numbers are how the founder, the rehearsal's "owner" and staff, and the test phone sign in until L2; any other number gets no code.
- L2, once accounts 2 and 3 exist: the vendor call, which sends the registered OTP template with its DLT template id (the hook receives only the code, so the function must reproduce the registered template text exactly), with its own unit tests; then the end-to-end test on staging with the founder's phone, and the hook registered on production.
- Secrets (the hook secret, the vendor key, the sender id, the template id) are function secrets set in the dashboard, never committed.
- Unverified, to settle on staging: the vendor's exact request (for MSG91, how a DLT template id maps to its "Flow" id, seen only in search results), whether `test_otp` numbers bypass the hook, and whether the project's SMS rate limit still applies.

## Vercel

- One Pro team, two projects on the same GitHub repository: `web` with root directory `apps/web` and `partner` with root directory `apps/partner`. Vercel skips the build of a project whose files did not change when the repository is on GitHub and internal dependencies are declared ([monorepos](https://vercel.com/docs/monorepos), 2026-08-11). The repository is owned by a user, not an organisation.
- Functions in `bom1` (Mumbai) for both projects; new projects default to `iad1` ([function regions](https://vercel.com/docs/functions/configuring-functions/region), 2026-08-11).
- Environment, per project, with the publishable key only (ADR 0011): `web` gets `GOG_DATA_SOURCE=supabase`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL=https://api.<domain>`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL=https://<domain>` and the store variables when they exist; `partner` gets `NEXT_PUBLIC_GOG_DATA_SOURCE=supabase`, the same URL and key, and `NEXT_PUBLIC_WEBSITE_URL=https://<domain>`. Preview deployments may point at staging.
- Domains: `<domain>` on `web` (an A record or Vercel's nameservers) and `partner.<domain>` on `partner` (a CNAME to that project's own target), per [adding a domain](https://vercel.com/docs/domains/working-with-domains/add-a-domain) (2026-09-16).
- After the first deployment: `partner.<domain>` answers with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, and its `robots.txt` disallows everything.

## The onboarding visit

Adapted from the partner research of 16 November 2026, with each step tied to the screen it uses. Until M5 is deployed, the visit stops after publishing and the cafe is listed only. A printed copy of this list works before `apps/partner` exists.

**Before the visit (about 20 minutes).** Pick targets: multi-branch brands first, and cafes already on Playo or District, which know partner dashboards (`marketplace_playbook.md`, `playo_product.md`). Create the draft in `/ops/new-cafe` from public information (map pin, hours, Instagram, any rate card) and treat every field as "to confirm", because public listings decay (`owner_player_voice.md` §4). Add any of the cafe's games that are missing from `games` (Supabase dashboard), so step 5 can be done on the phone: `set_cafe_games` refuses unknown ids, and the migration brings only 16. Bring two printed copies of the one-page partner terms, the paper form for business details and opt-ins, the founder's phone with both apps, and a second phone signed in to the player app as a test player (row 4a: an Android phone with an EAS preview build until L5).

**At the counter (10 to 15 minutes).**

1. Confirm who decides, and that this person is 18 or over and authorised to run the business.
2. Ask the fixed questions and note the answers in the founder's research log, not in the product: how many parties are turned away, or wait more than 30 minutes, on a weekend evening; which channels bring bookings (walk-in, phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, Playo, District, others); what software runs the floor; the busiest and emptiest hours; whether groups book ahead.
3. Show the board and the controls, then state the deal: ₹0, no exclusivity, walk-ins and other channels untouched, the cafe chooses what the app may sell, hide or leave any day, and the founder is the person to call. Promise no demand numbers.

**Walk the floor (20 to 30 minutes), editing the draft as you go.**

4. Stations (Stations screen): kind and the cafe's own tier names; specs; how many; labels exactly as numbered on the floor and in the cafe's software, so staff can copy app bookings into it; which stations to pledge; seats next to each other for squads and private rooms (in the description).
5. Games per platform (Games), and whether players must bring their own accounts (description).
6. Prices: the tier rate is the most the counter charges per station-hour in the hours the station is pledged, including any weekend premium. The counter may charge less, never more, and the description says when (weekday rates, hour packs). Do not pledge a console tier priced per controller until open decision 7 is decided: the app quotes per station, so a squad of four on one PS5 at ₹120 an hour would be quoted ₹240 for two hours against a walk-in price near ₹770 at GameBiller's group multipliers (`market_economics.md` §3), and a higher bill at the desk is the surprise that keeps first-timers away (`owner_player_voice.md`). List such a tier with its controller prices in the description.
7. Hours per weekday (Hours), including overnight and the real closing time; known holidays as closures (Settings, from M4).
8. House rules: the grace period for late arrivals (Settings), the no-show rule, age policy, school-uniform policy, ID, food and smoking (description).
9. Photos: the exterior and signage, the entrance and stairs, each tier, the console bays, the counter. No customers' faces and no minors in frame. Uploaded the same day, in the app from M6 or through the Supabase dashboard.
10. Amenities.

**Business details, people and consent (10 minutes).**

11. On paper, kept in the founder's private records outside git: trading and legal names, whether the business is registered, address, GSTIN or PAN where it has one, a grievance contact (open decision 9). The public WhatsApp number goes into the listing only if the cafe wants players to use it.
12. Licences are self-declared only: trade licence, the police licence for a place of public amusement, fire NOC. Never describe a cafe as safe or certified; at most, say which documents were on file on which date (`regulation.md` §3).
13. People: the owner's own mobile number, which must receive SMS because it becomes the login; a second owner, so a lost phone does not lock the cafe out (only the founder can remove an owner, so a second one is added now, not later by whoever is at the counter); the staff who will use the board.
14. In the app: the founder invites the owner's number as owner (Team). The owner signs in on their own phone, joins, opens Preview, checks the listing, and accepts the terms; the owner then signs the paper copy. An owner already signed in from another branch's visit reloads instead, or taps "Check for invitations". Staff are invited the same way. Owners sign out of any shared counter PC (open decision 1).
15. Separate opt-ins on paper: the use of photos, and partnership messages from the founder. The partner privacy notice (`/privacy`) is shown to the owner and staff. Alerts by WhatsApp or SMS are not offered in this iteration.

**Publish and set up the counter (10 minutes, plus waiting time).**

16. The founder publishes (Preview). The cafe is listed; app bookings are off.
17. Open the board on the counter PC or a staff phone and note which device the counter uses. Staff sign in, press "Sound on" and "Test sound", and "Keep screen on" where it is offered. Reload once to show staff the "Sound is off" banner they will see after any restart. On a phone, the board alerts only while it is on screen.
18. The test booking (from M5): set the notice to 30 minutes, pledge one station and switch app bookings on as the founder (Settings; an operator needs no approval for this); from the test phone (until L5, an internal EAS build of the player app pointed at production, row 4a), book the next slot the app offers. Staff hear the alert and mark it seen, and find it by its code. Staff can check it in from 30 minutes before its start, so finish the photos meanwhile; then staff check it in, move it to another station and complete it. Book a second slot and cancel it from the test phone, so staff see a player's cancellation; and have staff cancel a third as the cafe, with a reason, which the test phone and `/ops` then show.
19. If every step of the test passed, the founder approves app bookings (Settings, "Approve app bookings"; it needs the completed, checked-in test booking), and only then can the owner switch them on or off. If a step failed, leave the cafe unapproved and listed only. Choose how to start: listed only (switch off), or bookable on a small pledge, for example two stations per tier with two to three hours' notice, widened later; switch app bookings on only where a counter device keeps the board on screen through opening hours, otherwise the cafe stays listed. Set the notice back accordingly.
20. Leave a one-page "When an app booking arrives" sheet and the founder's number.

**After the visit.** The same day, finish the copy and photos and ask the owner to look at the preview again if anything changed. In the first week, watch `/ops` on the first weekend on an always-on device (a locked phone gets no alert), ring the squad of any cafe cancellation it lists, phone the cafe after its first bookings, and revisit after seven days to adjust pledges, notice and prices. If a cafe fails its players, withdraw its approval (app bookings off until the founder approves again) or suspend it (hidden until the founder lifts the suspension).

## Before the public player launch

Needed before L5 (ADR 0011), and built in M7 (`01-scope.md`): the deletion path (`delete_my_account` and Delete account in the player app, which Apple requires in an app that creates accounts), the website's page for deletion requests without the app (Google Play asks for a web link; no form, ADR 0004), and the final player privacy notice, which must also say what ADR 0017 shares. Counsel supplies the notice's text and the DPDP consent wording (row 8). The main DPDP duties are computed to start on 13 May 2027 (`regulation.md` §4); under-18s need verifiable parental consent, and nothing measures how many players are minors. On a personal Google Play account the closed test must have run for 14 days with at least 12 testers before production access is requested (row 9), so it starts in L0, not here.
