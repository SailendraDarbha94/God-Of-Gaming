# Size and unit economics of the gaming cafe market in India

Research date: 3 November 2026. Anything dated before 3 November 2024 is flagged STALE (possibly out of date).

How to read the source tags:
- **[analyst]** research firm or industry report. **[survey]** sample survey.
- **[company]** claim by the brand itself, unaudited. **[vendor]** blog by a software or equipment seller with something to sell. **[affiliate guide]** Gaming Nation, an SEO and affiliate-commission site whose editor also does SEO work for startups; it has internal inconsistencies, but where I could check it against cafe websites in Pune it matched.
- **[seller]** unverified business-for-sale listing on SMERGERS, which skews toward owners who want out. **[cafe site]** the cafe's own website. **[mill]** market-report mill. **[unsourced]** page gives no source. **[student]** learner case study.
- **[search only]** I saw this in a search result but could not open the page. All other links were opened.
- **[my calc]** arithmetic I did on the cited numbers. **[my estimate]** my own judgement.

## 1. How many gaming cafes are there in India, by city, and who has tried to count?

### Takeaway
Nobody has done a national census. The only rigorous counts I found are two city-level Google Maps screens from October 2026: Pune metro 103 (59 double-confirmed) and Gurugram 6. My own estimate is about 2,000 dedicated PC or console cafes nationally (range 1,000 to 3,600), and the widely repeated 15,000 figure has no source behind it.

- This excludes arcades and family entertainment centres, and micro-parlours with under 10 Google reviews. Including those could perhaps double the count, but they are poor aggregator targets.
- NVIDIA's roughly 350 in 2017 and Pune's 103 in 2026 suggest large growth, though the definitions differ (decent esports cafes versus anything with PC or console and 10 reviews).
- Gaming Nation's own Pune figure of 10+ versus its 103 screen shows its other city minimums undercount by perhaps 6 to 10 times.

### Gaps
- No national census, trade body or government count of gaming cafes was found. That is no evidence found, not evidence that none exists.
- Justdial city totals could not be read because the site refuses automated fetching. A person could get them in an hour by opening the category pages for the top 10 cities, or a Google Places API sweep using Pune's method.

## 2. Who plays, what do they own, and why go to a cafe rather than play at home?

### Takeaway
India has 500M+ gamers, but that number is irrelevant to this business: it is mobile-first, with PC and console called niche in every report, and cafe customers are overwhelmingly students and people in their teens and early 20s. The reasons for going to a cafe (hardware, ranked play, friends) are consistent across sources but rest on one 2017 NVIDIA data point and guide-level assertion, not recent surveys.

### Inferences
- [my calc] A ₹1.5 lakh PC equals 1,500 cafe hours at ₹100 an hour, or about three years at 10 hours a week. A ₹55,000 PS5 equals about 458 hours at ₹120. Cafes win on cash flow for students, not necessarily on lifetime cost for serious players.
- IDC's PC price rises cut both ways: home PCs get less affordable, but cafe capex rises too.
- The headline 500M+ gamer figure should not be used as the addressable market. The relevant base is PC and console players in cafe-served cities who are students or young earners. No source sizes this directly.

### Gaps
- No current count of PC gamers or console owners in India was found. Niko's India PC gamer numbers are behind its paid report, whose contents list has a section on esports, livestreaming and internet cafes ([Yahoo Finance summary](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/india-pc-mobile-console-games-102500121.html)).

- No India-specific PS5 installed base after 2022. Sony publishes global shipments only, as far as I could find.
- No recent survey on why players choose cafes; the NVIDIA 2017 data is the only quantified evidence.
- BCG and KPMG reports were not reached before the search budget ended. An [EGROW Foundation report](https://egrowfoundation.org/site/assets/files/2329/indias_booming_online_gaming_industry-_a_potential_powerhouse_final.pdf) I opened has no gaming-cafe content.
- Whether cafe spend is counted anywhere in FICCI-EY or Niko totals: no evidence found.

## 4. What do cafes cost to set up and run?

### Takeaway
A 15 to 25 seat PC cafe costs about ₹15 lakh to ₹25 lakh to set up, and blended occupancy of 25% to 40% is the realistic band, with peaks at 70% to 90% and weekday mornings near zero. Most franchisors claim payback of 5 to 24 months, but my model gives about 2.5 years at 30% occupancy, and seller listings show small cafes grossing only ₹25,000 to ₹80,000 a month.

| Blended occupancy | Revenue per month | EBITDA per month | Payback at ₹21.5 lakh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | ₹1.80 lakh | ₹38,000 | 57 months |
| 30% | ₹2.16 lakh | ₹71,000 | 30 months |
| 35% | ₹2.52 lakh | ₹1.03 lakh | 21 months |
| 40% | ₹2.88 lakh | ₹1.36 lakh | 16 months |

- EBITDA break-even is about 19% occupancy, but this excludes the owner's pay and hardware replacement. GameBiller's ₹2,200 per PC per month is ₹44,000 for 20 PCs, which leaves about ₹27,000 at 30% occupancy. That squares with vendor claims that 30% to 35% is the real break-even.
- Payback of 2 to 5 years is my realistic range for typical cafes. 12 to 24 months requires about 40% blended occupancy, which GameBiller says few floors reach.
- Revenue per station from listings [my calc]: ₹7,300 a month in Pune and ₹15,000 for a Bengaluru PS5, against GameBiller's modelled ₹13,000 for a PS5 at 30% occupancy. These line up. Pune implies about 3 billed hours per station per day, Bengaluru about 3.3 to 4.1, so about 25% to 35% occupancy.
- Small listed cafes gross only ₹25,000 to ₹80,000 a month. Per station they are at or a bit below vendor models (Pune ₹7,300 against CafeNex's implied ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 for 15 seats), so the vendor models are not wildly off per seat, but most cafes are smaller than the 15 to 25 seats those models assume. NVIDIA's ₹1.9 crore a year is about 3 to 60 times these listings, so treat it as vanity. Bigger 30 to 40 PC cafes must gross more, but none publish it.
- Five occupancy points are worth about ₹33,000 a month of contribution to this cafe, which is what an aggregator has to sell.

- No audited unit economics or named founder interview with real numbers was found. Business-press search could not be completed. No evidence found, not evidence that none exists.
- No published P&L for a 30+ station cafe. Refill24x7 (40+ stations per branch) and Intencity (80 PCs across three branches) would be the people to ask.
- No Indian per-kWh tariff or per-station power measurement. [GameBiller's power article](https://www.gamebiller.com/blog/power-backup-for-gaming-cafes-in-india) declines to give one.
- No hard data on staff pay in metros.

## 6. Is the market growing or shrinking?

### Takeaway
Probably growing in cafe count from a low base, with high churn. Every signal is indirect, as no time series exists, and the evidence comes mostly from sellers of software and franchises who benefit from a growth story.

## 7. Bottom-up estimate of aggregator revenue potential

### Takeaway
**All figures here are my estimates.** At maturity (about year 3 to 5), I estimate annual aggregator revenue of about ₹7 lakh (low), ₹1.3 crore (base) and ₹18 crore (high), which is about US$7,000, US$130,000 and US$1.8 million. On today's cafe count, a commission-only India aggregator is a small business, not a venture-scale one, unless it adds other revenue lines or the cafe universe grows several times over.

### Cited Findings

**Evidence on total visits per cafe (basis for bookings per day)**

**AOV build-up:** seats x hours x realised rate. Low 1.3 x 2 x ₹75, about ₹200 (tier-2, off-peak). Base 1.7 x 2.5 x ₹80, about ₹350. High 2.5 x 3 x ₹80, about ₹600 (metro groups). Excludes food, which cafes keep. A party-level AOV of ₹200 to ₹500 also matches the Playo case study range.

**Bookings check on a 20-seat cafe:** at 30% occupancy over 12 hours that is 72 station-hours a day, about 29 player sessions at 2.5 hours, about 17 parties at 1.7 players. So 0.5, 2 and 5 bookings are 3%, 12% and 30% of all parties. The base case adds about 3.5 occupancy points if entirely incremental. At the small cafes in the listings (10 to 30 visits a day) even 2 bookings is a high share.

**Top-down sanity check on total cafe floor spend:** low 1,020 cafes x ₹1 lakh a month, ₹122 crore a year. Base 2,000 x ₹1.5 lakh, ₹360 crore. High 3,640 x ₹2.5 lakh, ₹1,092 crore. So roughly US$12 million to US$110 million a year. Compare NVIDIA's ₹7,587 crore potential, and FICCI-EY's ₹6,300 crore for video games and esports in 2025, which I do not think includes cafe floor spend. App GMV is 0.45%, 2.8% and 10% of floor spend across the cases. The high case therefore means one in ten rupees spent in Indian cafes goes through the app. Read it as a ceiling.

**What has to be true for ₹10 crore of commission revenue:** at ₹350 and 10% (₹35 a booking), that is 28.6 lakh bookings a year, or 7,800 a day: 20 per cafe at 400 cafes, or 8 at 1,000 cafes. That exceeds the entire daily footfall at many small cafes. At ₹600 and 15% (₹90 a booking), it needs 3 per cafe per day at 1,000 cafes, which is about the high case.

**Single-city first year (illustration):** Pune, 30 cafes, 1 booking a day, ₹300, 10%: 10,950 bookings, ₹33 lakh GMV, ₹3.3 lakh revenue. This will not pay for a sales person.

**Key points for the founder**
- Base-case revenue per cafe (about ₹31,500 a year, ₹2,600 a month) sets a hard ceiling on what it can cost to sign up, train and retain a cafe. This is thin when cafes churn.
- **The peak paradox:** peak seats are already at 70% to 90% and don't need demand, while the quiet hours that need demand are already discounted 30% to 50%, so commission is lowest where the cafe's need is greatest. A player-side fee for guaranteed peak seats, or group reservations, may be a better wedge than a cafe-side commission on off-peak.
- **Three forces cap the take rate:** prepaid packs lock in regulars at ₹40 to ₹90 an hour; zero-commission software with booking exists at ₹499 a month, and GameBiller already has a consumer finder; WhatsApp plus a live seat map is free and good enough for cafes with loyal customers.
- **What supports a commission anyway:** base-case bookings, if fully incremental, add about ₹23,000 a month of contribution to a 20-seat cafe (8.5 station-hours a day at ₹80 plus food margin [my calc]) for about ₹2,100 of commission. A truly incremental-demand story sells, if the cafe can see it in data.
- Real upside lies beyond commission and I have not modelled it for lack of data: brand sponsorship at network level ([Next Level](https://next-level.gg/franchise) sells this; [Mumbai app](https://www.smergers.com/business/profitable-video-games-store-for-sale-in-mumbai-india/r5dff/) claims ads from fintech and accessory brands), tournament listing fees (entry ₹50 to ₹500 per [Gaming Nation](https://gamingnation.in/gaming-cafe-near-me/) and [PS5 guide](https://gamingnation.in/ps5-gaming-cafe-near-me/)), wallets and birthday or corporate group bookings. Hardware brands' cafe budgets (NVIDIA ran a cafe tournament in 2024) are the obvious lead, but esports sponsorship is shrinking.
- Non-Indian markets were out of scope. Playo's listing of venues outside India suggests the founder could later test whether cafe-dense countries change the picture.

**Main sensitivities:** bookings per cafe per day and cafes on platform each move revenue linearly and carry the most uncertainty. Universe count matters less than the signed-up count, since an aggregator would not reach saturation in any scenario.
