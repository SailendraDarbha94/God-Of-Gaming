# Gaming cafe management software and booking precedents outside India

Research date: 3 November 2026. All text is paraphrased. Tags used below:
- (search only) = seen only as search-result text; the page itself was not opened, or refused the fetch. Everything else was opened.
- (vendor) = company-claimed. (listing) = third-party software directory. (low quality) = content-farm, AI-style directory or anonymous self-media.
- (stale) = evidence older than two years at the research date, so before November 2024. Treat as possibly out of date.
- (undated) = the page shows only a copyright year; the figure is as fetched on 3 November 2026.

Method limit that affects every section: the shared WebSearch budget for this run ran out part-way through. After that I only opened pages already surfaced or obvious vendor domains. Indian startup names, Japanese chains other than Kaikatsu, and Korean software vendors got less searching than planned.

## Key question 1: Which cafe-management systems exist, with pricing, geography and features?

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and cheap. Dollar-priced products cluster at roughly $1 to $5 per PC per month, several products are free, and Russian vendors price separately in rubles. Reservation features are common but uneven: some are staff-only, some sit in higher tiers, and only some have a player-facing web portal or app.

### Inferences
- At a 30-PC cafe, global tools cost roughly $30 to $150 a month (my arithmetic from the published per-PC prices). Software revenue per venue is small, so a booking layer cannot assume large software margins.
- The market is mid-migration from Windows-era tools to cloud rewrites (HandyCafe's 2026 reissue is the clearest case). Expect integration targets to change.

### Gaps
- ggLeap's own price list and SENET's exact tier prices are not public on pages I could open. G2, Capterra and Software Advice returned 403.
- SENET's help centre also returned 403, so details of its booking module are from marketing pages only.
- No independent active-venue counts exist for any vendor. Every count is company-claimed, and units differ (clubs, seats, registered venues).
- I could not establish ggCircuit's ownership after FY2023. Search found no sale; that is no evidence, not evidence of none.

## Key question 2: Which are actually used in India, and are there Indian-made ones?

### Takeaway
I found no independent adoption data, so the question of actual use can only be partly answered. The visible evidence shows a young, fragmented layer of Indian-made tools plus iCafeCloud as the one foreign vendor with a visible Indian channel. Several Indian vendors already reach toward consumers, which directly overlaps this idea.

### Cited Findings

**Indian-made or India-first**
- **GameBiller.**
  - Free tier covers up to 5 stations after a 30-day trial. Premium is Rs 499 a month including GST, or $10 abroad ([site](https://www.gamebiller.com/), undated). A [Capterra listing](https://www.capterra.in/software/1110316/GAMEBILLER) describes the free plan as unlimited stations (search only), which conflicts.
  - Claims 1,700+ gaming centers, 4,900+ stations, 130,000+ invoices, 100+ countries ([site](https://www.gamebiller.com/), vendor).
  - Customers request seats from a public page, the venue's own link or a reception QR with no app or login, and the venue approves or rejects ([site](https://www.gamebiller.com/)).
  - PC Lock, PlayStation Lock via an Android TV app, smart-plug support, GST invoices and WhatsApp receipts ([site](https://www.gamebiller.com/)).
  - A consumer page offers finding turf, courts and gaming nearby, covering turf and pickleball, gaming cafes, play areas and sleeping pods. No venues, cities or counts are shown ([book page](https://www.gamebiller.com/book)).
  - Launched on Product Hunt in 2026, built with Supabase, Razorpay and Vercel, no reviews there ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/gamebiller)).
  - Trustpilot: 15 reviews, 12 from Indian reviewers, none verified ([Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.gamebiller.com)).
- **Olympus (Code Clutch Esports Labs, Bengaluru).**
  - 40+ gaming cafes in India and 10,000+ players on the app ([site](https://olympus.codeclutch.io), vendor, undated). Price not published.
  - The site says players can find nearby cafes, book a PC and log in remotely, and offers owners featured arena listings and push marketing ([site](https://olympus.codeclutch.io)).
  - The App Store text describes one account across the network, picking a free PC, QR sign-in, and passes bought on site with no in-app payment; it does not mention advance reservation ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/olympus-cc/id6736886622), versions from 31 Oct 2024). The two conflict on booking.
- **CafeSynk.**
  - Premium Rs 600 per PC per month in India, $6 elsewhere. Lite is free for Indian cafes, and a Biller tool is free ([site](https://cafesynk.com), undated).
  - Claims 18 countries. The cafe count is inconsistent on the same page (50+ versus 297).
  - Named venues include XGT in Chennai, Bangalore and Vellore and GGWP in Udaipur ([site](https://cafesynk.com), vendor).
  - Has a customer app and wallet recharges; seat booking is not described ([site](https://cafesynk.com)).
- **CafeNex.**
  - Rs 999 Basic and Rs 1,499 Pro with unlimited stations, QR login, in-seat ordering, wallets on Pro; 500+ stations and Rs 4.8M+ processed ([site](https://cafenex.com), vendor).
  - Launched in 2026 ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/cafenex)). Its launch listing promises seat booking ([Hunted](https://hunted.space/dashboard/cafenex)), but its site does not mention customer booking ([site](https://cafenex.com)). The two conflict.
- **CafeXP (Hyderabad).** Freemium, Rs 150 per hour usage pricing and a public API, per one directory dated 26 Sept 2026 ([Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/cafexp), low quality, unverified).
- **NoLag.** Described by a founder as a cafe operating system covering billing, wallet, timer and reporting ([LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/in/aashwijr), search only, self-reported).
- **PlayGG.** Jaipur; a job listing that gives no product detail ([Weekday](https://jobs.weekday.works/founder-s-office-at-playgg-4409795551)).

Launch is not confirmed ([portfolio](https://srisaikumarojjela.carrd.co/)). This is the closest match to the founder's concept that I found.

**Foreign vendors with some Indian signal**
- **iCafeCloud** has an India locale listing three local partners: Intencity in Pune, Samuel Electronic Sports Private Limited in Bangalore, and a partner in New Delhi. Prices are shown in USD ([India page](https://www.icafecloud.com/in/), undated). Its API docs include a Razorpay webhook handler ([API docs](https://dev.icafecloud.com/docs)). Its India blog post of 28 May 2026 gives no Indian counts ([blog](https://www.icafecloud.com/blog/browsing-center-software-for-india.htm), vendor).
- **Antamedia** carries rupee list prices of Rs 6,930 to Rs 55,930 on an Indian marketplace, with the newest review from 2019 ([Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/detail/antamedia-internet-cafe), undated, possibly stale).
- **IZI** publishes Hindi guides aimed at Indian cafes ([guide, 12 July 2026](https://izi.is/wiki/hi/use-cases/gaming-cafe-kaise-khole/)), but its site names clubs only in Russia, UAE, Saudi Arabia and Morocco ([site](https://izi.is/en)).
- **HandyCafe and SENET** have Capterra India listings ([HandyCafe](https://www.capterra.in/software/1110051/HandyCafe), [SENET](https://www.capterra.in/software/1020278/senet), both search only). A listing is not evidence of use.

### Inferences
- GameBiller's figures imply about 2.9 stations per center (4,900 divided by 1,700, my arithmetic). That suggests sign-ups or very small shops rather than active full-size cafes, so the 1,700 should not be read as cafe market share.
- Indian price anchors run from free, through about Rs 500 to Rs 1,500 flat per month, up to Rs 600 per PC. A 30-PC cafe would pay Rs 18,000 a month on CafeSynk Premium versus Rs 499 on GameBiller (my arithmetic). Paid software alone will be hard to sell.
- With no dominant billing vendor visible, API connectors alone would reach few Indian cafes. A cafe-side tool of the founder's own, or manual availability, probably has to be the default.
- GameBiller and Olympus are the competitors to watch: both already control the PC lock and are moving toward consumer discovery.

### Gaps
- No survey, registry or owner poll of software use among Indian cafes was found. Reddit-targeted queries returned no threads, so owner opinion is missing.
- I could not verify any Indian venue running ggLeap, SENET or Gizmo. That is no evidence found, not evidence of absence.
- Search budget ran out before dedicated searches on Olympus, PlayGG, GameOrbit or other Indian booking apps. Their stage, funding and traction are unknown beyond the pages cited.

Live integration is technically possible. iCafeCloud and Gizmo publicly document endpoints for both occupancy and reservations; ggLeap has a documented public API with paid rate tiers and says it supports bookings. I found no availability webhooks and no aggregator partner programme anywhere, and every API uses per-venue credentials, so each cafe must opt in individually.

### Inferences
- Onboarding is a per-cafe credential handover, not a single partner contract. A connector design needs a per-cafe secret store and expiry monitoring. iCafeCloud also needs a fixed outbound IP, and its one-key limit means a cafe regenerating its key breaks the connection.
- Without webhooks, availability must be polled. ggLeap's free tier averages about 6.9 requests a minute (10,000 divided by 1,440, my arithmetic), so polling every 15 to 30 seconds per venue fits. Treat polled data as a hint and the create-reservation call as the real check.
- Identity must be reconciled per venue. Provision venue-side members or use guest bookings; matching on phone number, as Langame does, looks like the practical join.
- Gizmo's API sits on each venue's server, so reaching it may need network changes on site. This is my reading of the docs, not something stated there.
- Vendor conflict is a real risk: Langame, SmartShell, IZI, Olympus and GameBiller run their own consumer channels and may have little reason to feed a rival aggregator.
- Suggested connector order for India: iCafeCloud first (Indian partners, Razorpay support, public docs), then Gizmo and ggLeap only when a specific cafe needs them.

### Gaps
- I made no live API calls, since that would need accounts. Endpoint behaviour, rate limits in practice and availability latency are untested.
- ggLeap's endpoint list is unread. A capped scan of 260 of its public script files found no spec location.
- Vendor terms on commercial third-party use were not found for any vendor.
- No formal aggregator or partner API programme was found. iCafeCloud's local partners appear to be sales channels, which is my inference. Direct questions to vendors would settle this.
- Korean (Pica, Gameto) and Chinese (Shunwang) software APIs were not established.

## Key question 4: How do China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam, Indonesia and the Philippines handle this?

### Takeaway
Where booking apps exist at scale, they are usually built by the billing-software vendor or the chain itself: Pica in Korea, Kaikatsu in Japan, and Langame in Russia and the CIS, which I add because it is the closest precedent found. China is the exception, because horizontal super-apps sell cafe vouchers and esports hotel rooms rather than live seats. Korea's norm still appears to be walk-in. No comparable app turned up for Vietnam, Indonesia or the Philippines.

### Cited Findings

**Russia and CIS (added: strongest precedent)**
- Langame's consumer app launched in 2022. It shows clubs in 26 countries, supports booking and purchases at more than 2,400, and has over 2 million downloads ([CNews, 22 July 2026](https://zoom.cnews.ru/news/item/698358), company-sourced).
- Its site showed 4,503 clubs in search on the fetch date, filterable by price, rating, type (PC, PlayStation, VR, racing sim), distance and map, with PC specs and promos ([home](https://langame.ru/), vendor). So the directory is well beyond the bookable subset, by these two figures.
- Booking through the app is included in every subscription, aggregator traffic is sold as a benefit, and no commission is mentioned ([software page](https://langame.ru/software)).
- Top-ups go to the specific club and refunds are handled there. The app needs its own registration, then syncs with club accounts by phone number ([App Store RU](https://apps.apple.com/ru/app/langame/id1642484175), developer replies). Rating 4.8 from about 2,300 ratings on that store.
- The Langame+ update added profiles, friends, Steam integration, live LAN status, a global ranking based on activity at clubs with mobile booking, double bonus cashback on app top-ups, and a lower commission on Steam wallet top-ups ([CNews](https://zoom.cnews.ru/news/item/698358)).
- Langame's founder claims over 60% of Russian gaming seats, and the same piece cites over 3,000 venues ([Habr, 4 July 2025](https://habr.com/ru/post/925096), self-reported, possibly promotional).
- Owner reviews praise support, stability and staff anti-theft controls, and criticise the admin UI ([CRMindex](https://crmindex.ru/products/langame/reviews), self-reported).

**China**
- About 77,000 internet-service venues and 5.43 million terminals at Q4 2023. The same report says Shunwang's core products cover 70%+ of the market and 96%+ of cities in 2023, and that it works with 2,000+ local partners ([Open Source Securities, 20 Sept 2024](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202409201639954543_1.pdf), broker report citing an industry association and company data, stale by weeks).
- Shunwang's chairman put esports hotels at under 20,000 with revenue of 50 to 60 billion yuan, and said their customers spend over 10 times as much as cafe users. Its CEO said attracting and reactivating customers matters most to cafes, and framed marketing services as their next step ([Cailian Press, 30 July 2022](https://www.cls.cn/detail/1086211), company claims, stale).
- Esports hotels are booked as hotels in the Meituan app. In early 2021 searches ran nearly five times 2019 levels ([China Daily, 4 Feb 2021](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202102/04/WS601bab4ca3101e7ce973e7ef.html), stale).
- By 15 March 2022 Meituan and Dianping showed a notice on all esports hotels that merchants may refuse gaming equipment to minors ([The Paper, 17 March 2022](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_17164810), stale).
- Internet cafes appear on Meituan and Douyin mostly as group-buy vouchers such as all-night passes and 3-hour trials, usually valid 7 or 30 days, with unredeemed ones commonly auto-refunded ([Sina](https://www.sina.cn/news/article/nikknsc1953211.html), [Sina](https://sina.cn/detail/niizerm1360513), search only, low quality). A July 2026 piece claims Meituan coupons cover over 90% of cafes ([Sina](https://sina.cn/detail/nikciqa8686424), search only, low quality, unverified). I cannot be sure which claim sits in which of these articles, because they refused fetching.
- Meituan's deposit design for restaurants, which is adjacent: a deposit confirms the table, a merchant who fails to honour it refunds double, and merchants set a free-cancellation window ([Tiger Brokers news, 12 Feb 2026](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/1115132277)).

### Inferences
- The pattern is that whoever controls the PC lock owns booking. Langame shows how far that goes: the aggregator is bundled into software pricing with no visible commission, so software fees and adjacent services appear to fund it. That is my reading, since its revenue split is not public.

- Kaikatsu's remaining-seats-by-category display is a coarser, cheaper availability model than a per-seat map.

## Key question 5: What about US and European chains and booking tools, and were there aggregator attempts?

### Takeaway
Western LAN chains have contracted sharply, with Belong and Platform the clearest cases and SideQuest close behind, and bookings there sit inside each venue's own portal. Horizontal tools charge flat fees with no commission. I found no dedicated Western LAN-centre booking aggregator at scale; the one small attempt I found (Lanslot) has an unknown outcome.

### Cited Findings

**LAN-centre chains**
- **Belong.**
  - In June 2021 owner Vindex planned 500 US arenas in five years, on top of 25 in the UK, with reservations through its platform and an app in development ([ICSC, 23 June 2021](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/esports-company-plans-500-arenas-across-u.s), stale).
  - US operations were suspended in July 2023, with most US staff laid off ([Esports Advocate, 3 July 2023](https://esportsadvocate.net/2023/07/exclusive-belong-layoffs/), stale). A Texas site closed that August ([Community Impact, 21 Aug 2023](https://communityimpact.com/grapevine-colleyville-southlake/business/grapevine-gaming-center-to-shut-down-after-almost-2-years-in-operation/), stale).
  - UK venues peaked at 23 by this source (ICSC said 25, a minor conflict) and have mostly closed; the few remaining moved into Sports Direct as GAME entered administration ([Esports News UK, updated 2 Feb 2026](https://esports-news.co.uk/?p=178626)).
- **SideQuest.** Three of its four UK venues were set to close on 30 Nov 2025 with no stated reason, and customers holding bookings were told to contact support. It began as the esports cafe brand Wanyoo ([Esports News UK, 26 Nov 2025](https://esports-news.co.uk/?p=169981)).
- **Platform (London).** Operating company entered creditors' voluntary liquidation in April 2024 ([Esports News UK](https://esports-news.co.uk/2024/04/17/platform-gaming-bar-enters-liquidation), stale).
- **Contender Esports.** Franchise chain with 10 branches on ggLeap and ggRock in March 2022, selling drop-in fees, memberships, tournaments and concessions ([ggCircuit blog](https://blog.ggcircuit.com/?p=6054), stale). A 2025 profile lists 14 locations ([Sharpsheets](https://sharpsheets.io/?p=54465), search only, unverified). I found no closure reports, and no later confirmation of its software.
- **Owner view.** A US venue founder writing for ggCircuit calls relying on gaming alone one of the biggest mistakes, and recommends food, pinball, merchandise and game cards ([blog, 14 Sept 2025](https://blog.ggcircuit.com/?p=6552), anecdotal).

**Arcade and VR tools**
- **SpringboardVR:** $30 per station per month, month to month; content billed per minute played; embeddable reservations using Square and Stripe ([FAQ](https://www.springboardvr.com/faq), undated). Sold by Vertigo Games to SynthesisVR, announced 2 Feb 2025 ([GameQuarter](https://www.gamequarter.be/nieuws/springboardvr-wordt-overgenomen-door-synthesisvr/)). A forum operator called its booking too basic and paid about $100 a month for Checkfront ([forum](https://community.springboardvr.com/d/1163/2), search only).
- **SynthesisVR:** a no-subscription access tier, paying for content and optional modules including booking ([VR Collective](https://thevrcollective.com/product/synthesisvr/)). 300+ centres and 600,000+ reservations in 2021 ([Auganix](https://www.auganix.org/fantasy-360-to-acquire-location-based-virtual-reality-content-store-technology-engine-provider-synthesis-vr/), search only, stale, vendor).
- **Bookeo:** $14.95 to $119.95 a month for tours and activities, with no commission on payments ([pricing](https://www.bookeo.com/tours/pricing/)).
- **PlayPro (France):** free up to 300 reservations a month, then EUR 39, 119, 199, with no sales commission ([listing](https://slashdot.org/software/p/PlayPro/)).
- **Funbutler:** internet-cafe booking pages with group flows and deposits, 500+ venues across activity types ([site](https://funbutler.com/industries/internet-cafe-booking-software), vendor).
- **Anolla:** free plan, usage-based fees, deposits, waitlists, dynamic pricing, and a marketplace listing for gaming services ([site](https://anolla.com/en/gaming-center-software), vendor).

**Aggregator attempts**
- **Lanslot (France).** A founder who closed a gaming center began it in June 2018 to geolocate gaming events and venues. It had a 2019 beta and about 140 donors ([WVU Tech](https://wvutech.edu/news/2019/12/19/golden-bear-dispatch-chedli-ben-hassine-17), search only, stale). The www.lanslot.com domain did not resolve on 3 Nov 2026, which is weak evidence only; it may have used another domain.
- **Anolla** is a horizontal marketplace, with gaming-venue scale unknown ([site](https://anolla.com/en/gaming-center-software)).
- A search for failed or closed LAN-centre directories and booking startups surfaced nothing else.

### Inferences
- Commission-free flat pricing is the norm in horizontal booking software. An aggregator charging commission must therefore justify itself with net-new customers, not tooling.
- Fragile venue economics create supply risk. SideQuest's stranded bookings show why refunds and venue-closure handling need designing from day one.
- SynthesisVR's figures imply about 2,000 reservations per site per year, or 5 to 6 a day (my arithmetic). VR is scheduled and group-based by nature, so it may not translate to walk-in LAN play.
- Reasons for Western closures are mostly unstated. Do not conclude that booking demand itself failed, or that Indian cafes will follow the same path.

### Gaps
- No credible data on Western booking share, no-show rates or commission willingness was found.
- Owner-community evidence is thin: searches surfaced vendor material rather than owner forums, and no Discord or Reddit discussion was reached.
- Other chains (Allied Esports, Simplicity Esports, Beyond Gaming) were not researched in depth. The outcome of Lanslot is not established.
- Whether the Synthesis VR purchase by Fantasy 360 in 2021 completed was not checked.

## Key question 6: Which features should an Indian aggregator borrow, and what do these products reveal about what cafes truly need?

### Takeaway
Booking is rarely what these products lead with. They lead with billing control, PC lockdown, prepaid balances and support, and booking exists mainly to allocate scarce stations at peak. The most borrowable ideas are a request-to-book mode, category-level availability, deposit rules, phone-number identity, spec-level discovery, and owner tools that bring repeat customers.

Evidence on what cafes actually value:
- **Control and leakage.** Langame owners most often praise support, stability and tracking of staff actions that makes cash theft harder ([CRMindex](https://crmindex.ru/products/langame/reviews), self-reported).
- **Peak-hour scarcity.** Gizmo's docs frame reservations as a fix for no free stations at peak ([docs](https://docs.gizmopowered.net/docs/articles/booking)).
- **Customer acquisition.** Shunwang's CEO says attracting and reactivating customers matters most ([Cailian Press, July 2022](https://www.cls.cn/detail/1086211), stale), and Langame sells aggregator traffic as a benefit ([software page](https://langame.ru/software)).
- **Prepayment reduces no-shows.** IZI says clients who paid show up at a significantly higher rate, with no figures given ([playbook](https://izi.is/wiki/en/hall/handle-no-show/), vendor).
- **Non-gaming revenue.** A venue founder argues gaming alone is not enough ([blog, Sept 2025](https://blog.ggcircuit.com/?p=6552), anecdotal).

### Inferences
Suggested reading for the report writer; these are my judgement, not sourced facts.
- **Supply tiers.** Tier 1: listing plus phone, WhatsApp and request-to-book, no integration. Tier 2: the founder's own lightweight station manager with PC and TV lock, giving true live availability. Tier 3: connectors to iCafeCloud, then Gizmo and ggLeap.
- **Availability honesty.** Start with category-level counts (for example, PCs free or PS5 free), and offer a seat map only where the lock is controlled. Without the lock, walk-ins cause double-booking.
- **Monetisation.** Plain commission looks weak given flat-fee norms and Langame's bundled model. Consider a player-side convenience fee (ggLeap's fee shows venues accept this concept), featured listings, and off-peak passes that fill empty hours.
- **Money flow.** Copy Langame's per-club wallet, or at least decide early who holds the funds. A platform-held wallet raises refund and regulatory questions that were not researched here.
- **Data model.** Bookable units should be stations of mixed type: PC, console, room, plus a rig's specs and installed games. Keep a per-venue time zone and policy object from day one, even with a single-country launch.
- **Cafe value first.** Lead the cafe pitch with new-customer attribution, repeat-customer tools and empty-hour filling. Peak-hour booking alone helps only when the cafe is already full.
- **Build on the walk-in reality.** Treat booking as an add-on to walk-in flows, not a replacement. Korea's guide and Gizmo's framing both point this way, though the evidence is indirect.

### Gaps
- No data on how much revenue or how many sessions come through booking at any cafe, in any country.
- Evidence on cafe needs is mostly vendor-authored or vendor-hosted; no independent survey of owners was found.
- Whether Indian cafes accept deposits, and whether Indian players pay a convenience fee, cannot be answered from this research. Direct interviews would address it.
- Legal questions (minors, licensing, wallet regulation in India) were not researched in this strand.
- Source-handling notes: handycafe.com/llms.txt is a machine-oriented vendor index, so I treated it as vendor marketing data. No page contained instructions that I acted on.
