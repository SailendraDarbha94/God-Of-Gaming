## 1. Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025: status, and does it touch offline cafes or esports?

### Takeaway
The Act has been in force since 1 May 2026, and a constitutional challenge is pending in the Supreme Court. Its text regulates online games, not premises, so ordinary paid seat time at a cafe looks outside it. No source says so directly. Paid-entry prize tournaments are the feature most likely to fall inside it, and the aggregator could be exposed itself, not only the cafe.

### Cited Findings
- Status: Parliament passed the Act on 21 Aug 2025, assent followed on 22 Aug 2025, and the Act and Rules took effect on 1 May 2026 (Supreme Court Observer case page, updated 2 Jul 2026) — [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/challenge-to-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025/)
- The Rules were notified in the Gazette on 22 Apr 2026 and took effect on 1 May 2026 (news, 24 Apr 2026) — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-online-gaming-rules-notified-may-1-major-changes/). King Stubb & Kasiva (law firm, 12 May 2026) says there is effectively no compliance window for the core prohibitions and penalties — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025-and-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-rules-2026)
- Source conflict: one search-result summary attributed an 18-month phase-in ending 13 May 2027 to this Act. The law-firm piece above contradicts that, and the date matches the DPDP timeline, so it looks like a mix-up. Treat it as unverified.
- Litigation: Head Digital Works v. Union of India, T.C.(C) No. 133/2025, is pending before CJI Surya Kant and Justices Bagchi and Pancholi. Issues include Articles 14 and 19(1)(g) and State legislative competence — [SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/cases/challenge-to-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025/). On 5 Aug 2026 the Court agreed to hear the challenges and told parties to complete pleadings. The report gives no final hearing date. A separate PIL says about 2,000 apps pose as social or e-sports games — [Telangana Today, 5 Aug 2026](https://telanganatoday.com/supreme-court-to-hear-challenges-against-online-gaming-act-pil-seeks-ban-on-gambling-apps)
- Regulator: the Online Gaming Authority of India started work around 1 May 2026. It is chaired by a MeitY Additional Secretary and focuses first on e-sports registration and grievances. A portal is live and registration is said to last 10 years (exam-prep summary of an Economic Times report, 2 May 2026; secondary, medium quality) — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-05-02/the-economic-times/polity-and-governance/egaming-regulator-takes-charge-focuses-on-esports-registrations). Wikipedia's reference to a National Online Gaming Commission appears out of date — [Wikipedia, search result only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_and_Regulation_of_Online_Gaming_Act,_2025)
- Definitions (TaxGuru's reproduction of the Act, close paraphrase):
  - Online game, s.2(1)(f): a game played on an electronic or digital device, run as software over the internet or another technology enabling electronic communication.
  - Online money game, s.2(1)(g): a game of skill, chance or both where the user pays fees, deposits money or puts up other stakes hoping to win. E-sports are excluded.
  - Online social game, s.2(1)(i): no staking, and it may charge a subscription or one-time access fee that is not a stake.
  - User, s.2(1)(m): anyone accessing an online game.
  - None of the core terms mentions physical venues. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/promotion-regulation-online-gaming-act-2025.html)
- E-sport, s.2(1)(c): must be part of multi-sports events, involve organised competition under set rules, be recognised under the National Sports Governance Act 2025 and registered, and be decided mainly by skill. It may charge entry or administrative fees and pay performance-based prizes, and must involve no betting or stakes by anyone — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/promotion-regulation-online-gaming-act-2025.html)
- Prohibitions and penalties:
  - s.5 bars offering, aiding, abetting or inducing online money games. Penalty under s.9(1): up to 3 years, ₹1 crore, or both.
  - s.6 bars advertising in any medium. Penalty under s.9(2): up to 2 years, ₹50 lakh, or both.
  - s.7 bars banks and anyone else facilitating payments. Penalty under s.9(3): up to 3 years, ₹1 crore, or both.
  - s.10 makes s.5 and s.7 offences cognizable and non-bailable. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/promotion-regulation-online-gaming-act-2025.html)
- King Stubb & Kasiva describes money games as those where users expect monetary or other enrichment. It says s.6 has no knowledge-based carve-out, so influencers and creators are in scope. Under Rule 19, banks must verify the Certificate of Registration before facilitating transactions for registered social games or e-sports (law firm, 12 May 2026) — [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-act-2025-and-the-promotion-and-regulation-of-online-gaming-rules-2026)
- Registration: voluntary for social games and mandatory for e-sports, and officials said some formats could still be examined as money gaming (trade press, 25 Mar 2026, before notification) — [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/gaming-news/online-gaming-rules-eased-social-games-registration-voluntary-under-prog-act-2025-ws-e-93213.htm). Final Rule 12 keeps registration for e-sports, and Rule 8 adds a determination step — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/04/223-online-gaming-rules-notified-may-1-major-changes/). The Vision IAS summary says registration binds platforms that brand tournaments as eSports and that a Sports Ministry certification is also needed — [Vision IAS](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-05-02/the-economic-times/polity-and-governance/egaming-regulator-takes-charge-focuses-on-esports-registrations)
- Industry concern: Skyesports' CEO said recognition is limited to titles featured in multi-sport events and could exclude games that drive the ecosystem (trade press, 4 Oct 2025) — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/gaming-news/e-sports-industry-welcomes-online-gaming-act-flags-restrictive-recognition-rules-81967.htm)
- Premises-level spillover: a Karnataka High Court order of 2 Dec 2025 on a members' club included limits on digital and online real-money gaming, covering payment methods and network access — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/70665926/)

### Inferences
- Seat time: a booking fee buys time on a machine with no prospect of winning, so it does not look like a stake under s.2(1)(g). The Act creates no premises licence. This is my reading; no source confirms it.
- Tournaments are the danger zone. A paid-entry event with a cash or other prize on an online title that is not a recognised, registered e-sport fits the plain words of online money game. Because of the multi-sport-event condition, most cafe titles may not qualify as e-sports.
- The aggregator's own exposure comes from the verbs in ss.5 to 7. Listing, promoting or collecting entry fees could count as aiding, advertising or facilitating, and two of those offences are non-bailable.
- Options to put to counsel: free-entry events with sponsor-funded prizes, and an early decision to leave prize tournaments out of the first release.
- Avoid the word esports in product and marketing copy unless the title and organiser are registered.
- The definition reaches any technology enabling electronic communication, so LAN play may be an online game for this purpose. Purely offline single-player console play seems outside it. No source addresses either point.

### Gaps
- The official text could not be opened (eGazette certificate error, India Code 403), so definitions come from TaxGuru's reproduction and law-firm summaries.
- No law firm, regulator or court source addressing LAN cafes, offline tournaments or venue liability was found. This is absence of evidence only.
- No list of titles recognised under the National Sports Governance Act was found.
- No report of a Supreme Court hearing date or outcome after 5 Aug 2026 was found, and the search budget ended before this could be retried.

## 2. State and municipal licensing for video game parlours and gaming zones; raids and closures

### Takeaway
There is no national regime. A 1995 Supreme Court ruling upheld police licensing of video game parlours and remains the baseline, but the local picture has diverged. Delhi dropped police licensing in June 2025, Maharashtra reportedly exempted parlours in 2016, and Chennai's rules still set hours and under-15 limits. Karnataka's High Court now imposes detailed conditions on clubs. I found one clear recent enforcement example on point (Kalyan, November 2025), and it involved minors, no licence and no fire safety.

### Cited Findings
**National baseline**
- M.J. Sivani v. State of Karnataka (Supreme Court, 17 Apr 1995; old but a precedent, flagged for currency):
  - Video game operators must get licences under police and amusement rules, and conditions are valid.
  - Games involving stakes, such as tokens exchangeable for money, are gaming.
  - Authorities must give reasons when refusing a licence. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/694951/)

**Delhi**
- A notification of 19 Jun 2025 on the Lieutenant Governor's orders cancelled Delhi Police licence orders for seven business types including video game parlours. Local bodies (MCD, NDMC, Cantonment Board) regulate them instead (news, 30 Jun 2025) — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/delhi-7-businesses-no-longer-need-police-license-2025-lg-issued-notification-enn25063006899)
- Source conflict on the statutory basis: ETV Bharat cites s.28(1) of the Delhi Police Act 1978, while another search result cited s.28(2) read with s.4.
- Civic bodies become the point of authority for health-trade licences. A unified portal for health, fire and pollution approvals is planned, and police keep their law-and-order role (news, 26 Jun 2025) — [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/News/explained-delhi-scraps-police-permission-for-restaurants-and-hotels-what-it-means-for-hospitality)
- The earlier Delhi regime, for comparison: a one-year licence from the Delhi Police licensing unit with police, traffic, electrical and fire NOCs and an MCD health trade licence, fee ₹65 (consultant page, 7 Sep 2024; consultant-reported, older than two years, and overtaken on the police licence) — [CRSPL](https://crspl.in/blog/video-game-parlour-license)

**Maharashtra and Mumbai**
- Om Sai Video Game Parlour, Navi Mumbai (Bombay High Court, WP 9023/2013, 28 Oct 2013; possibly stale): the licence fell under s.33(1)(w) of the Bombay Police Act 1951. A refusal based on parking and vague law-and-order fears was quashed as unreasonable, and the licence was ordered — [LawText](https://lawtext.in/judgement.php?bid=62921)
- A March 2016 notification under the Maharashtra Police Act reportedly exempted video game parlours from the NOC requirement. The Bombay High Court said some entertainment parlours are not covered and that police can still act under the gambling law (news, dated 29 Jan with no year shown; possibly stale) — [Mumbai Live](https://www.mumbailive.com/en/civic/no-more-need-for-noc-to-operate-video-game-parlours-82888)
- Kalyan East raid: police raided a game zone in a rented room, found eight minors said to be skipping school and tuition, no extinguishers and no licence or permission, and arrested three operators. Sections were not named (news, updated 27 Nov 2025) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/kalyan-police-raid-illegal-joystick-jungle-game-zone-arrest-three-and-rescue-minors-skipping-classes)

**Karnataka and Bengaluru**
- Sivani covers the Mysore (now Karnataka) Police Act 1963 and the Bangalore City licensing order of 1989 — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/694951/). A practitioner note says the police commissioner licenses video game, billiards and snooker parlours (forum answer, about 10 years old; possibly stale) — [Kaanoon](https://www.kaanoon.com/76535/starting-a-snooker-hall-business)
- Karnataka High Court writ orders on recreation clubs follow a template. Orders of 2 Jun 2016, 30 May 2024 and 2 Dec 2025 impose:
  - CCTV with 15-day retention;
  - member ID cards;
  - no non-members or guests;
  - no chance games, stakes or betting;
  - police inspection powers.
  - The December 2025 order also bars prize money and commissions, and adds a compliance committee and fire, building, electrical and noise compliance. Lawful activity is protected, but police powers are preserved. The 2024 order concerned skill games rather than video games. — [2016, possibly stale](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/83157250/), [2024, possibly stale](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/127636043/), [Dec 2025](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/70665926/)
- Bengaluru's Central Crime Branch raided three parlours and arrested 71 people for betting on video games (Deccan Herald, date not shown; search result only, likely old) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2F71-held-raids-video-game-2131094)

**Telangana and Hyderabad**
- The Hyderabad Police permissions page lists 28 licences and NOCs, mostly food and hospitality plus explosives and cinematography, and none mentions gaming or amusement (official page, read 3 Nov 2026). This does not prove no licence exists — [Hyderabad Police](https://hyderabadpolice.gov.in/permission.html)
- A wiki says a municipal corporation or police may issue the licence, cites no statute, and is partly template text (low quality, 29 Apr 2025) — [Wikiprocedure](https://wikiprocedure.com/index.php/Telangana_-_Apply_for_Video_Gaming_Parlour_License)
- The Hyderabad raids I found were card or betting cases, not video cafes. Example: a Marredpally gaming house, 16 arrested, 24 Nov 2018 (possibly stale) — [Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/amp/nation/crime/251118/hyderabad-gaming-parlour-raided-16-arrested.html)

**Tamil Nadu and Chennai**
- Rules under the Chennai City Police Act 1888 (ss.36 and 39), rule 46, which the database labels section 46 (undated legal database text; verify currency):
  - the Commissioner grants the licence after considering public objections;
  - open only between 10 a.m. and 10 p.m.;
  - no more than three people per machine;
  - children under 15 in school uniform cannot play from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. except on Sundays and holidays or with a parent;
  - no gambling or chance machines;
  - a visitor register of name, age, address and entry and exit times for police;
  - building, electrical and fire rules, and no smoking. — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/148461945/)
- Rule 47 lets a Sub-Inspector or above enter during working hours — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/185145067/)
- Sivani also covers the Madras City Police Act 1888 — [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/694951/). The Tamil Nadu Gaming and Police Laws (Amendment) Act 2021 changed related definitions (search result only; effect on parlours not assessed) — [Legitquest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/tamil-nadu-gaming-and-police-laws-amendment-act-2021/C2E3)

**General**
- A consultant page says licences come from municipal or police authorities and run one year, at ₹5,000 to ₹50,000 a year. These figures are consultant-claimed and unverified; the page is promotional, dated 2 Jul 2025 — [IP Global](https://ipglobal.in/video-game-parlour-license-in-india/)
- Not a cafe case: a Kangra raid on a rented house over online betting and mule accounts (news, October 2026; search result only) — [The420](https://the420.in/kangra-online-gaming-racket-busted-birta-police-raid/)

### Inferences
- The licence belongs to the cafe, but the marketplace needs a city-by-city document schedule. A single national onboarding checklist will be wrong somewhere.
- Delhi now looks trade-licence-led. Chennai still looks police-led, with conditions that could be built into the booking engine: hours, an under-15 school-hours flag, and a digital visitor register.
- Chennai's 10 p.m. close would conflict with overnight LAN sessions. Check this before promoting them in Tamil Nadu.
- The Karnataka club orders bar guests and non-members, so a walk-in commercial cafe probably cannot rely on that route. It should expect the public amusement licence path.
- Minors in school hours, a rented room and no fire equipment look like the enforcement trigger, judging from one case. One case does not establish a pattern.
- A digital register holding name, age and address raises the DPDP issues in section 4.

### Gaps
- No primary Delhi gazette text was opened, and no MCD licence category for gaming parlours was found.
- Maharashtra's current position is unclear. I have neither the 2016 notification nor the year of the Mumbai Live report, and the Kalyan police's no-licence claim sits awkwardly with the reported exemption.
- No Mumbai-specific age or timing rule was found. A search summary that seemed to show one was actually the Chennai text.
- No Telangana statutory basis was found for gaming cafes. This is absence of evidence only.
- It was not confirmed whether Bengaluru's 1989 order has been amended or replaced. A 2024 order refers to a state-level 1970 order.
- No age or timing rules were found for Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru or Hyderabad. The search budget ended before further queries, such as gaming parlour sealed by city, could run.

## 3. Fire and safety after the May 2024 Rajkot game zone fire

### Takeaway
Gujarat is the only state where I found game-zone-specific rules. It has draft and model safety rules plus building-regulation amendments, all from 2024 and all older than two years. Rajkot and Ahmedabad show that holding a licence or a fire NOC does not prove a venue is safe. A marketplace has good reasons to collect documents, but should not imply it has certified safety.

### Cited Findings
- Gujarat High Court, Suo Motu v. State of Gujarat, WPPIL 34/2024, order of 13 Jun 2024 (primary):
  - TRP operated from June 2021 with only a police permission under s.33(x) of the Gujarat Police Act, which covers ticket sales.
  - It had no s.33(w) licence, no building use permission, and fire NOC and structural documents were queried only in May 2024.
  - The Court recorded prima facie connivance and ordered a fact-finding inquiry.
  - It said police issuing a ticket licence had to check that premises norms were met. — [LiveLaw PDF](https://livelaw.in/pdf_upload/gujarathighcourtorderdatedjune13-545218.pdf)
- The Rajkot police commissioner's affidavit said the licences were booking licences under s.33(1)(x), not s.33(1)(w) (news, 5 Jun 2024) — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/gujarat-high-court/gujarat-high-court-rajkot-police-commissioner-files-affidavit-clarifies-licensing-trp-gaming-zone-gujarat-police-act-259682)
- The death toll varies by source and date: about 25 (order above), 27 (draft-rules report below), 28 ([Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/owners-of-5-gaming-zones-booked-as-amc-seals-52-units-over-safety-norms)), 33 in an early report ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/india/rajkot-gamezone-fire-gujarat-government-orders-statewide-closure-of-gaming-zones)), and at least 36 in another early report ([South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/karnataka-government-orders-inspection-of-safety-measures-at-gaming-zones-in-bengaluru)).
- Closures: the state ordered all gaming zones shut and checked for fire equipment and NOCs (26 May 2024) — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/india/rajkot-gamezone-fire-gujarat-government-orders-statewide-closure-of-gaming-zones). The Court was told zones were halted pending inquiry (27 May 2024) — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/gujarat-high-court/gujarat-high-court-criticizes-authorities-over-unauthorized-gaming-zones-orders-stringent-action-and-accountability-258984). On 4 Jul 2024 the Court refused to modify the 27 May order for a permitted Surat venue that said it held the required permits and had about 120 staff. The order stood until further orders — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/gujarat-high-court/gujarat-high-court-dismisses-plea-against-gaming-zone-ban-rajkot-trp-game-zone-fire-case-264668)
- Draft Gujarat Amusement Rides and Gaming Zone Activities (Safety) Rules 2024 (news, 13 Jun 2024):
  - scope covers amusement rides and indoor gaming zones;
  - the licensing authority is the police commissioner or district magistrate, with licences of at least two years;
  - certifications cover fire safety, evacuation plans, building use, structural stability, health, sanitation and electrical work;
  - occupancy limits apply, and fire and evacuation drills run every 90 days;
  - third-party insurance of at least ₹10 lakh per patron for death or permanent disability, ₹7.5 lakh for partial disability and ₹2 lakh for hospitalisation. — [Vibes of India](https://www.vibesofindia.com/?p=336761)
- Model rules (2 Aug 2024): ride safety and inspection committees are appointed by commissioners or collectors, and renewal needs a certified inspector's certificate — [Top Indian News](https://www.topindiannews.com/national/gujarat-introduces-model-rules-to-regulate-gaming-zones-after-rajkot-fire-tragedy-news-21621). The Home Department sent them to commissioners and collectors to notify (search result only) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/gujarat/rajkot-fire-tragedy-gujarat-govt-frames-model-rules-to-regulate-gaming-zones-amusement-rides-3134821)
- Building regulations (5 Sep 2024): CGDCR amendments set road width, plot area, height, parking, separate entry and exit, emergency exits and refuge areas. Venues must display BU certificates, fire NOCs and other permits. Existing buildings need revised permissions, and penalties apply — [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/trp-game-zone-fire-gujarat-govt-brings-new-planning-regulations-to-ensure-public-safety)
- Paper is not proof: Ahmedabad teams inspected 244 properties on 29 May 2024 and acted against units that had a fire NOC but no working fire systems. The headline says 52 units were sealed, while the article's breakdown lists 16 — [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/gujarat/owners-of-5-gaming-zones-booked-as-amc-seals-52-units-over-safety-norms)
- Criminal exposure: FIRs against owners of 18 illegal zones (search result only) — [Outlook](https://outlookindia.com/national/rajkot-game-zone-fire-latest-update-gujarat-police-file-firs-against-illegal-gaming-zone-owners). Bail was refused to TRP co-owners in January 2025 (search result only) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fgujarat%2Frajkot-game-zone-fire-gujarat-high-court-grants-bail-to-three-accused-3381078)
- Karnataka: on 26 May 2024 the Deputy CM told the BBMP chief to inspect gaming zones in Bengaluru, including those in malls. No outcome is reported — [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/karnataka-government-orders-inspection-of-safety-measures-at-gaming-zones-in-bengaluru)

### Inferences
Would a marketplace want to verify? Yes, in my view, for commercial and reputational reasons more than because any source I found requires it.
- Collect these, with expiry dates and automatic delisting on lapse: fire NOC, building use or occupancy permission, the police or municipal licence, electrical certificate, insurance, and an occupancy figure.
- Rajkot shows the licence class matters, so check what the licence actually covers. Ahmedabad shows a NOC can sit alongside dead equipment.
- For public wording, say documents on file as of a given date. Avoid safe or certified. A claim the platform cannot stand behind may be treated as misleading under the Consumer Protection Act (penalty summary in section 5).
- Basements, temporary tin structures and single-exit rooms deserve manual review. This comes from the Rajkot and Kalyan facts, and is not a legal requirement I can cite.
- Whether a small PC or console cafe counts as a gaming zone under Gujarat's rules is open. If it does, the insurance and 90-day drill requirements become onboarding items in Gujarat.

### Gaps
- No final gazette text or notification date for the Gujarat rules was confirmed.
- The rules' definition of gaming zone was not seen.
- No post-Rajkot game-zone rules were found for Delhi, Maharashtra, Telangana or Tamil Nadu. Search returned no relevant results for Delhi or Maharashtra and the budget ended before follow-ups. This is not evidence that none exist.
- The current status of the 27 May 2024 order is unknown.
- No authority was found on whether a booking marketplace can be liable for an incident at a listed venue. This was not specifically searched.

## 4. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

### Takeaway
The core duties start around mid-May 2027. Plan to 13 May 2027, the earlier of two computed dates. Under-18s need verifiable parental consent, and tracking and targeted ads are barred. The exemption schedule covers healthcare, education and childcare, with nothing for entertainment or booking. Penalties for children's-data breaches reach ₹200 crore. The Data Protection Board is constituted but reported as not yet functional.

### Cited Findings
- Timeline: G.S.R. 843(E) (commencement) and G.S.R. 846(E) (Rules) set phases counted from publication. These are Board provisions immediately, Consent Manager registration at one year, and main duties at 18 months. G.S.R. 892(E) is a December 2025 corrigendum (independent reference site, checked 28 Oct 2026) — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)
- Source conflict on the date. The printed Gazette masthead says 13 Nov 2025, while the eGazette file code and a PIB backgrounder say 14 Nov. So Consent Manager registration falls on 13 or 14 Nov 2026 and main duties on 13 or 14 May 2027. That site advises planning to the earlier dates — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/blog/dpdp-commencement-date-13-or-14-november); [PIB backgrounder, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf). Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas uses 14 Nov 2026 and 14 May 2027 (law firm, 21 Nov 2025) — [SAM](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
- Shortening proposal: MeitY reportedly floated a move from 18 to 12 months around 22 Jan 2026. Reports differ on whether it covers all fiduciaries or only Significant Data Fiduciaries. No amendment had been published as of October 2026 — [Privacy Labs, 8 Oct 2026](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts); [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)
- Board status: applications were invited on 6 May 2026, and no members were reported appointed as of 1 Aug 2026 — [Privacy Labs](https://theprivacylabs.com/blogs/dpdp-13-november-2026-what-starts). In C R Neelakandan v Union of India (Kerala High Court, 7 Oct 2026), the Centre's counsel said the Board is constituted but not functional. The Court expected it to function within 8 weeks, which is an expectation and not a direction (as reported by the independent site) — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/timeline)
- Section 9 (mirror of the Act): a child is anyone under 18.
  - 9(1) requires verifiable parental consent before processing.
  - 9(2) bars processing likely to harm a child's wellbeing.
  - 9(3) bars tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children.
  - 9(4) allows exemptions by rules.
  - 9(5) lets the Centre notify an age above which a verifiably safe fiduciary is exempt. — [dpdpa.com](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html)
- Rule 10: the fiduciary must use appropriate measures and due diligence to check the person claiming to be the parent is an identifiable adult. It can rely on reliable details it already holds, details provided voluntarily, or a virtual token from an authorised entity, with Digital Locker optional for the parent. The site's commentary says the rule's age check targets the parent and does not set out how to verify the child's age — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/rules/10)
- Law-firm view for contrast: King Stubb & Kasiva says simple age-confirmation screens are no longer enough and recommends age verification at account creation (14 Nov 2025). This goes further than the rule's text as paraphrased above — [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- Rule 12 and Fourth Schedule: Part A covers clinical and mental health establishments, allied healthcare, educational institutions, creches and day care, and child transport providers. Part B covers legal functions, subsidies and benefits, email accounts, real-time location for safety, blocking harmful content and ads, and confirming a user is not a child. No entertainment, retail or booking category appears — [Fourth Schedule](https://dpdprules.org/rules/fourth-schedule); [Rule 12](https://dpdprules.org/rules/12)
- Penalties (Act Schedule): up to ₹250 crore for security safeguards; ₹200 crore for breach notification; ₹200 crore for s.9 children's duties; ₹50 crore for other breaches — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/act/schedule); [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- Other operational duties:
  - One-year retention of personal data, traffic data and logs (Rule 8) — [dpdprules.org](https://dpdprules.org/rules/8)
  - Three-year inactivity erasure applies only to e-commerce entities with at least 2 crore registered users, online gaming intermediaries with at least 50 lakh, and social media with at least 2 crore. Marketplace sellers are excluded — [Third Schedule](https://dpdprules.org/rules/third-schedule)
  - Breach notice to all affected people regardless of materiality, and 90 days for grievances — [SAM](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)
  - A published contact point and a 90-day response limit on rights requests — [PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)

### Inferences
- If minors are a real share of customers, the cleanest launch design is adult-only accounts with a neutral age screen, and under-18 bookings made by a parent from the parent's own account. That keeps the child's data minimal and may reuse Rule 10's registered-parent route. Counsel should test it.
- Treat under-18 and unknown-age users as excluded from ad SDKs, behavioural analytics, retargeting and lookalike audiences. Apply the same rule to the promotional website's pixels. The Google Play policy in section 9 pushes the same way.
- Campaigns aimed at school or teen audiences, including influencer campaigns, need review before mid-May 2027 because of s.9(3).
- Cafes will probably be separate fiduciaries for their own registers, so contracts should allocate roles. A digital visitor register holding age and address makes the platform a processor or fiduciary of sensitive-feeling data. Decide which before building it.
- Launching before May 2027 is not a reason to skip this, since retrofitting consent and age flows is harder than building them first.
- The evidence for many customers being minors in this research is anecdotal: Kalyan (eight minors) and Rajkot (reports of many child victims). I did not find a measured share.

### Gaps
- The official Rules PDF could not be opened (MeitY 403), so rule text comes from an independent reference site that cites the Gazette and from law-firm summaries. Check it against G.S.R. 846(E) before relying on it.
- No guidance from MeitY or the Board on age assurance for general consumer apps was found.
- No source addresses gaming cafes or booking apps specifically.

## 5. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 and the 2023 dark-patterns guidelines

### Takeaway
There is a new development the founder should know about. Amendment Rules notified on 9 September 2026 take effect on 1 January 2027 and make dark-pattern compliance binding, with an annual self-audit and a displayed certificate. They also add ranking, sponsored-listing, prior-price and helpline duties. CCPA has already fined platforms for drip pricing and basket sneaking. No opened source names a convenience fee case, but the logic points to all-in pricing from the first screen.

### Cited Findings
- G.S.R. 789(E), Gazette of 9 Sep 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027 — [TaxGuru text](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/consumer-protection-commerce-amendment-rules-2026.html); [LexComply, 7 Oct 2026](http://lexcomply.com/blog/dark-patterns-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-compliance-guide-6ac68b344325c)
- New or changed duties, per the TaxGuru reproduction and [SCC Online, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-explained/):
  - Rule 4(15): comply with the 2023 Guidelines, self-audit yearly, and display a certificate. No method, scope or auditor qualification is set — [LexComply](http://lexcomply.com/blog/dark-patterns-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-compliance-guide-6ac68b344325c)
  - Rule 4(5): grievance acknowledged in 48 hours, a copy of the complaint given, resolved within one month.
  - Rule 4(7): National Consumer Helpline convergence becomes mandatory. Khaitan lists registration forms, an MoU, IT integration and a nodal officer — [Legal 500, 5 Oct 2026](<https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/consumer-protection/consumer-protection-(e-commerce)-(amendment)-rules-2026-additional-compliances-for-e-commerce-entities>)
  - Rule 4(11)(c): no misleading manipulation of search results. Rule 4(12): sponsored listings clearly labelled.
  - Rule 4(13): a price reduction must show the prior price, meaning the lowest in the preceding 30 days. Rule 4(14): the seller's name on the invoice must match the platform name's font size.
  - Rule 5(3)(a): seller details shown before purchase. Rule 5(3)(c): refund and related information.
  - Rule 5(3)(f): main ranking factors and their relative weight published in plain language. Poovayya says this does not require disclosing algorithms — [Bar & Bench, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-from-consumer-disclosure-to-platform-governance)
  - Rule 5(6): express consent for certain consumer-data uses. Rule 5(7): no bundled fees for services unrelated to the platform, with loyalty and membership programmes excepted.
  - Rule 6(5)(j): sellers display identifiers such as GSTIN or MSME numbers.
- Baseline: Rule 4(9) requires consent through an explicit, affirmative action, and sellers are outside the definition of e-commerce entity under Rule 3(1)(b) — [Triumvir Law, 1 Oct 2026](<https://triumvirlaw.com/insights/decoding-the-consumer-protection-(e-commerce)-(amendment)-rules-2026>). Automatic consent, including pre-ticked boxes, is prohibited — [Tuli & Co on Legal 500, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/consumer-protection/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview)
- Guidelines: notified 30 Nov 2023; they cover all platforms systematically offering goods or services in India, plus advertisers and sellers. Drip pricing includes revealing parts of the price late, and AZB notes non-optional fees common to most users should appear in the displayed price. Marketplaces are not liable for price fluctuations caused by third-party sellers or factors beyond their control (law firm, 6 Dec 2023; older than two years, but the Guidelines are now incorporated by the 2026 Rules) — [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/a-new-dawn-for-dark-patterns/). Drip pricing also covers charging more at checkout than was disclosed — [Tuli & Co](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/consumer-protection/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview)
- Enforcement: a written Rajya Sabha reply reported on 6 Aug 2026 put penalties at about ₹20 lakh in total — [New Kerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/govt-says-ccpa-imposes-rs-20-lakh-dark-948.htm). Zepto was fined ₹7 lakh for adding handling charges and membership fees at checkout. Physics Wallah was fined ₹5 lakh, Anuj Jindal ₹3 lakh and FirstCry ₹2 lakh — [ChannelIAM, 6 Aug 2026](https://en.channeliam.com/2026/08/06/ccpa-action-dark-patterns-digital-platforms-india/). FirstCry reportedly added taxes at the final stage despite tax-inclusive display — [Lexibal, 7 Aug 2026](https://lexibal.com/ccpa-dark-patterns-platforms-fined/)
- BookMyShow, the closest comparator: it was directed to remove a pre-selected ₹1 donation as basket sneaking — [ChannelIAM](https://en.channeliam.com/2026/08/06/ccpa-action-dark-patterns-digital-platforms-india/). Source conflict: a Business Standard headline says CCPA fined BookMyShow (search result only, page refused) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/ccpa-fines-indigo-zepto-bookmyshow-dark-patterns-quick-commerce-penalty-126080600580_1.html). New Kerala says that for BookMyShow, IndiGo and SpiceJet the action was corrective intervention, not penalties — [New Kerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/govt-says-ccpa-imposes-rs-20-lakh-dark-948.htm). Neither page I opened mentions its convenience fee.
- Advisory: on 5 Jun 2025 CCPA asked platforms to self-audit within three months — [Tuli & Co](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/consumer-protection/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview)
- Penalties per a legal blog: disobeying a CCPA discontinuance order, up to six months, ₹20 lakh or both; misleading advertisements under s.89, up to two years and ₹10 lakh for a first offence, rising to five years and ₹50 lakh. Its illustrations of false urgency on a booking site and a pre-checked ₹1 movie-ticket donation are guidance, not findings (27 Sep 2026; secondary) — [LawWeb](https://www.lawweb.in/2026/09/when-buy-now-becomes-trapped-now.html)

### Inferences
- The aggregator is very likely a marketplace e-commerce entity and the cafe a seller. The Rules and Guidelines refer to services, though I could not open the 2020 definition itself.
- A convenience fee charged on every booking is a non-optional charge common to most users. Show it in the first displayed price rather than adding it at checkout. Zepto is the nearest analogy.
- Rule 5(7) seems aimed at unrelated bundles, so a fee tied to the booking itself probably falls outside it. Still disclose it clearly.
- Off-peak pricing and strike-through prices meet Rule 4(13). Any announced reduction needs a 30-day lowest price per comparable slot, and the data model should keep price history from day one.
- Paid placement for cafes must be labelled, and ranking factors published.
- Pre-ticked add-ons (snacks, extra time, donations, insurance) should never ship. Low-seats counters must reflect live inventory, and no fake countdowns.
- Budget for the helpline integration and a recorded annual self-audit, even as a small company. No size threshold appeared in what I read, though I did not confirm that none exists.

### Gaps
- The official 2020 Rules could not be opened (Consumer Affairs site refused the connection). Their definition of e-commerce, the Rule 5 safe harbour wording, and any cancellation-charge rule are not confirmed here.
- No order or advisory naming a convenience fee as drip pricing was found. Consumer-commission cases on convenience fees were not searched because the budget ended.
- The official Gazette PDF of G.S.R. 789(E) was not opened. TaxGuru and TaxHeal reproduce it.

## 6. GST on a booking aggregator

### Takeaway
Cafe services now look like 18%. The often-cited 28% mall gaming zone ruling relied on entry wording removed in October 2021. Section 9(5) does not list recreation, so the platform should not be deemed the supplier for seat bookings, but food pre-orders could change that. TCS at 0.5% applies if the platform collects the money for registered cafes, and the platform must register regardless of turnover.

### Cited Findings
- Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate), 17 Sep 2025, effective 22 Sep 2025 (primary): at serial 34 it changes item (ii) to 2.5%, items (iiia) and (iv) to 20%, and adds a recognised-sporting-event explanation. It does not change items (iii) or (vi) — [Notification text](https://www.gstindiaonline.com/pages/gstlegislation/GST-Nfy/Central%20Tax%20Rate/2025/15-2025-CTR.pdf)
- Consolidated heading 9996 (tax reference site, updated 8 Mar 2026): item (iii) theme parks, water parks, joy rides, merry-go-rounds, go-karting and ballet at 9% CGST; item (iiia) casinos, race clubs and IPL-type events at 20% CGST; item (vi) other recreational services at 9% CGST. Since 1 Oct 2021 (Notification 06/2021), the amusement-facilities wording no longer appears in (iiia) — [GST Gyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/cgst-rate-on-recreational-cultural-and-sporting-services-heading-9996). Those CGST figures match totals of 18% and 40% elsewhere (search result only) — [Taxaj](https://www.taxaj.com/gst-sac-code-rates-finder)
- Older ruling: Bandai Namco India (Maharashtra AAR, 8 Apr 2019; upheld by the appellate authority, reported 21 Nov 2019) held a mall family entertainment centre to be an amusement facility taxed at 28% under entry (iiia) (older than two years, possibly superseded) — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gaming-zone-operated-mall-premises-attracts-28-per-cent-gst.html); [Amusements & Attractions, 3 Jul 2019](https://www.amusementsandattractions.com/amusements/news/indian-fec-tax-challenge-rejected)
- Secondary sources conflict: one guide says gaming zones generally remain at 18% (search result only) — [Busy](https://busy.in/gst-rates/gaming-industry/). An undated Q&A says 28% (search result only; likely stale) — [Taxlok](https://www.taxlok.com/view-query-answer/query_id=16989)
- The 40% slab covers casinos, race clubs, IPL-type admission, betting and online money gaming from 22 Sep 2025 (search results only) — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/tax/ipl-tickets-casino-entries-lottery-and-betting-to-attract-40-gst-from-september-22/article-180684)
- TCS: 0.5% in total (0.25% CGST plus 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) from 10 Jul 2024, down from 1% — [Masters India, 24 Jul 2025](https://www.mastersindia.co/blog/gst-implications-ecommerce-operator-guide/). It applies when the supply is made through the operator and the operator collects the payment. The base excludes s.9(5) services and same-month returns and counts registered suppliers' taxable supplies. Deposit is due within 10 days after month end, with GSTR-8 reporting — [TaxGuru, 23 Jul 2025](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/cart-compliance-deep-dive-e-commerce-gst.html). One CA firm's page still mentions 1% in one passage, which looks like stale text — [Rajput Jain](https://carajput.com/blog/taxability-under-gst-for-e-commerce-sale-of-services/)
- Registration: an operator required to collect TCS must register regardless of turnover under s.24(x) — [Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/blog/gst-implications-ecommerce-operator-guide/). Service suppliers selling through an operator are exempt below ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in special category states), citing Notification 65/2017 (CA firm page, undated; primary not opened) — [Rajput Jain](https://carajput.com/blog/taxability-under-gst-for-e-commerce-sale-of-services/)
- Section 9(5) list: passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping and restaurant services, with no recreation or gaming entry — [Masters India](https://www.mastersindia.co/blog/gst-implications-ecommerce-operator-guide/); [Rajput Jain](https://carajput.com/blog/taxability-under-gst-for-e-commerce-sale-of-services/)

### Inferences
- Plan on 18% for cafe seat time, whether under item (iii) or the residual item (vi). The 2019 ruling's reasoning rested on wording that has since gone. No post-2021 gaming zone ruling was found, so get a CA's written view.
- Section 9(5) should not apply to seat bookings. Adding food pre-orders through the platform could bring restaurant services into s.9(5), making the platform liable for that tax. Combo packages of seat plus snacks need separate treatment.
- A pay-at-venue model avoids TCS on that booking, because the operator does not collect the consideration. It loses payment control and raises no-show problems. A prepaid model brings TCS and GSTR-8 duties.
- Some small cafes may be unregistered. Check how TCS and invoicing work for them, because the base counts registered suppliers.
- The convenience fee and commission are the platform's own supplies and should carry GST. See the gaps below for the rate.
- The cafe's GST handling also interacts with the all-inclusive price display in section 5.

### Gaps
- No opened source states the GST rate or SAC on platform commission or convenience fees. It is probably 18% on residual business services, and Notification 15/2025 touches that entry's wording but does not state a rate there. This is unverified.
- Income-tax TDS on e-commerce operators (s.194-O and its successor) was not verified. Masters India does not cover it.
- Primary CBIC notifications 15/2024, 17/2017 and 65/2017 were not opened. CBIC's site could not be reached at the URL tried.
- I did not confirm that s.9(5) has no additions after July 2025.

## 7. Payments: marketplace splits, RBI payment aggregator rules, holding funds, refunds

### Takeaway
RBI's September 2025 Master Direction treats a marketplace as a merchant of a payment aggregator (PA), and a non-bank PA needs authorisation and ₹15 crore net worth. A startup should not be pooling and paying out cafe money from its own account. Razorpay Route and Cashfree Easy Split are the obvious rails, and their refund mechanics leave the platform carrying some recovery risk if a cafe's share has already gone out.

### Cited Findings
- RBI Master Direction, 15 Sep 2025 (primary; opened):
  - Merchant includes a marketplace, para 4(f).
  - A PA may not carry on marketplace business, para 10(b).
  - A PA must ensure a marketplace it onboards takes payments only for sellers on its platform, para 13(f).
  - Funds due to a merchant go to that merchant's bank account, para 13(g).
  - Non-bank PAs need authorisation and must be companies, para 5.
  - Net worth is ₹15 crore at application and ₹25 crore by the end of the third financial year, para 6(a).
  - Collected funds sit in a separate escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank, para 16(a).
  - No fixed settlement period such as T+1 is prescribed; timelines must be stated in the merchant agreement. — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896)
- Third-party settlement is limited: a debit to a third party on a merchant's direction is allowed only for merchants above ₹40 lakh annual turnover (or ₹5 lakh export turnover), and the third party must be the payee who interfaces with the payer — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896); [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- Small cafes: merchants at or below ₹40 lakh turnover get alternative due diligence with PAN or Form 60, contact point verification and valid documents. Onboarding from 1 Jan 2026 must follow the Direction — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896); [Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf)
- Refunds: to the original method unless the payer instructs otherwise (para 10(f)), and the dispute mechanism must include refund timelines (para 8(a)). No number of days is set — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896). A 2019 RBI circular sets failed-transaction reversal limits, such as T+5 for UPI merchant payments with ₹100 per day compensation, and does not treat merchant refunds as a category (20 Sep 2019; older than two years) — [RBI TAT circular](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693)
- Razorpay Route (docs read 3 Nov 2026):
  - Linked accounts complete a KYC form with business and bank details.
  - Transfers can settle in the next slot, on a date, or be held. A hold can be indefinite.
  - A refund comes from the primary account, so a reversal must be created manually or by automatic setting. Reversals can be partial.
  - A Refund Credits pool exists, and the linked account must directly interface with customers. — [Route dashboard docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/dashboard); [Route API](https://razorpay.com/docs/api/payments/route.md)
- Cashfree Easy Split (docs read 3 Nov 2026):
  - KYC depends on vendor and business type, and production access is through an account manager.
  - Instant, standard and on-demand settlement.
  - A vendor share can be deferred for at most 45 days, after which it settles to the merchant, not the vendor.
  - Maximum 5 vendors per transaction.
  - Refunds after a split are debited from merchant and vendor balances in proportion and applied to the next payout. — [Easy Split FAQ](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/faq); [Overview](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/overview.md)

### Inferences
- Avoid collecting into a company current account and paying cafes later. It resembles aggregating for others without authorisation, and the capital bar makes authorisation unrealistic at this stage. Counsel should confirm where the marketplace-as-merchant model ends.
- Use a short hold until session completion or the cancellation window closes, then release. Route's indefinite hold is technically possible, but a long hold is harder to defend. Cashfree's 45-day cap and its default of settling to the merchant need careful handling in failure cases.
- Set a clear refund policy for no-shows, cafe cancellations, machine failures and overruns, and map it to reversal logic. Keep a reserve or Refund Credits balance, since a cafe with low balances may not cover reversals.
- Small cafes should be able to onboard through alternative due diligence, which helps a long-tail supply strategy.
- Reconcile payment splits with invoices and TCS (section 6) from the start.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from RBI's list that Razorpay and Cashfree are authorised PAs. The list URL tried returned an error. Verify this before integrating.
- The Payment and Settlement Systems Act text was not reviewed.
- Pricing for Route and Easy Split was not reviewed.
- No RBI enforcement case against a marketplace holding seller funds was searched.

## 8. Game licensing: commercial-use limits, aggregator exposure and enforcement in India

### Takeaway
Every major consumer licence I read limits use to personal or non-commercial purposes. Steam is the only one with a formal cafe programme that I could confirm. Riot has run regional programmes elsewhere, and I found no official route for Sony or Microsoft consoles. These contracts bind the cafe rather than the aggregator, but the aggregator's own use of logos and game art is a direct issue. I found no evidence of publisher enforcement in India, but the dedicated search could not run.

### Cited Findings
- Steam Subscriber Agreement (revised 10 Sep 2026): content is licensed for personal, non-commercial use unless expressly allowed (§2.A). No commercial exploitation, rental or leasing without Valve's written consent (§2.G). Accounts are strictly personal and may not be shared or charged for or used to enable others' commercial use (§1.C). It has no cafe language — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/subscriber_agreement/)
- Steam PC Café Program:
  - It is the official route for cafes and public venues, with a free licence to offer Steam at registered locations.
  - Commercial licences are floating and pooled, so 20 PCs with 10 licences serve 10 at once.
  - Patrons may use their own Steam accounts and libraries.
  - Only titles whose publishers opted in are available, under a separate Cafe Distribution Addendum.
  - No offline mode, and VAC bans can cost commercial licences or programme membership.
  - Sign-up requires a legal name matching bank and tax records. Non-US corporations and sole proprietorships are accepted, but non-US partnerships taxed at partner level are not. India is not named. — [Program](https://partner.steamgames.com/pccafe); [Licensees](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sitelicense/licensees); [FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sitelicense/licensees/faq); [Publisher](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sitelicense/publisher); [Sign-up](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/sitelicense/licensees/signup)
- Sony:
  - Software Usage Terms grant a personal licence for private, non-commercial use (s.1.4). Renting or making available to others (s.1.4(a)) and public performance (s.1.4(g)) need prior written consent. India is not named, and I read it as falling in the EMEA region — [Software Usage Terms](https://www.playstation.com/en-in/legal/software-usage-terms/)
  - PSN Terms, v12 (April 2026): no commercial use of the services, systems or digital products, no charging others, and no public performance without the IP owner's consent (s.24.9). Store items are for private home use unless stated (s.18.2). No account sharing (s.8.3.4 to 8.3.5) — [PSN Terms](https://www.playstation.com/en-in/legal/psn-terms-of-service/)
- Microsoft Services Agreement (published 30 Jul 2026, effective 30 Sep 2026): Xbox Services are for individual, non-business use (s.13.a.i). Digital goods are for personal, non-commercial entertainment, with no public performance or display (s.13.i) — [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-in/servicesagreement). I found Microsoft's terms but no official cafe route. A vendor page says Microsoft's agreement prohibits commercial venue use for Game Pass (vendor, 31 May 2026, marked as possibly outdated) — [IZI](https://izi.is/wiki/en/setup/licenses-windows/)
- Riot Terms (modified 1 Dec 2024): an individual, non-commercial licence (s.3.1). Any business use needs express written consent (s.7.1(17)), the services are for domestic and private use (s.14.1(2)), trademark use needs a written licence (s.3.2), and account sharing is barred (s.1.5). Cafes and tournaments are not addressed — [Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/terms-of-service). Riot's legal page bars commercial fan projects involving a business entity unless licensed — [Riot Legal](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal)
- Riot has run cafe programmes outside India, such as the TNC partnership in the Philippines (July 2024; search result only, page refused) — [ABS-CBN](https://abs-cbn.com/sports/2024/7/15/league-of-legends-riot-partners-with-tnc-for-pc-cafe-program-2010)
- Source conflict: a vendor page says club use of Riot titles is generally permitted or not explicitly prohibited, which sits poorly with the business-use clause above — [IZI](https://izi.is/wiki/en/setup/licenses-windows/)
- EA User Agreement (14 May 2026): a personal, non-commercial licence with no commercial exception, and a PC game can launch on at most five machines in a rolling 24 hours — [EA](https://www.ea.com/legal/user-agreement). EA FC community tournament guidelines (undated, © 2026):
  - entry fee at most $20 or local equivalent, covering costs only;
  - total prizes at most $10,000 per calendar year;
  - no EA logos or marks, and a non-affiliation notice is required;
  - no gambling, alcohol, tobacco or crypto sponsors;
  - events must be small and non-commercial. — [EA FC guidelines](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-pro/ea-sports-fc-community-guidelines)
- India evidence is thin: a 2009 Indian forum thread on opening a Mumbai PS3 cafe asked if a Sony licence was needed and got no clear answer (forum, very old) — [TechEnclave](https://techenclave.com/t/business-case-for-a-ps3-cafe/154046). A UK forum poster reported that Sony said commercial use was not permitted while hourly cafes still operated (forum, undated; search result only) — [UK Business Forums](https://www.ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/gaming-cafes.142100/post-1124730)

### Inferences
- Many Indian cafes probably run outside these terms, particularly on consoles. Treat this as a contract and account-ban exposure for venues. Whether it amounts to copyright infringement in India is a separate question for counsel.
- Aggregator exposure is indirect for venue licensing, since the aggregator is not party to those licences. It is direct for its own marketing: game logos, box art and screenshots on the app and website, and copy that promotes hourly play of named titles.
- Practical controls:
  - plain-text game lists supplied by the cafe rather than publisher art;
  - venue warranties and an indemnity on software licensing;
  - a takedown process;
  - an optional Steam PC Café field that can be shown as fact;
  - no account-sharing features.
- Publisher complaints may reach the platform through app stores under Apple 5.2 (section 9) even if nothing happens in Indian courts.
- A cafe-sponsored FC tournament should follow EA's caps. Those caps also interact with section 1's entry-fee issue.
- The Steam partnership exclusion may matter for Indian partnership-firm cafes. Check how Valve applies it in India.

### Gaps
- Enforcement: the search for Sony or Microsoft notices and legal action against Indian cafes could not run (budget exhausted). I found no reports, but this is a weak negative.
- I did not find Riot's community competition rules for Valorant or League of Legends, or whether Riot runs anything in India.
- I did not find a Sony or Microsoft commercial cafe licence. Absence is not proof; a direct written query to each company would settle it.
- Copyright Act 1957 ss.14 and 51 could not be opened (copyright.gov.in certificate rejected, India Code 403). Whether permitting a place to be used for infringement reaches a cafe or a marketplace is unverified, as is intermediary safe harbour under s.79 of the IT Act. Indian case law was not checked.

## 9. App store rules: payments for physical services and age ratings

### Takeaway
Both stores exempt physical services from their own billing, so cafe bookings can run on an Indian payment gateway without store commission. Digital extras that unlock app features do not get this treatment. Age rating is likely modest for a pure booking app, but Google's rules on mixed audiences and Apple's rules on children's data line up with DPDP.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Guideline 3.1.3(e): physical goods or services consumed outside the app must use other payment methods, such as Apple Pay or card entry, not in-app purchase. Guideline 3.1.1: features, subscriptions and in-game currencies inside the app must use in-app purchase (page read 3 Nov 2026; no date shown) — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Google Play Payments policy, section 3: payments primarily for physical goods or services are exempt. Examples include gym memberships, airfare and live event tickets. Bookings are not named. In-app digital items and subscriptions generally need Play billing, and India is not named for alternative billing (read 3 Nov 2026; no effective date on the page) — [Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)
- Apple age ratings (iOS 26-era page, undated):
  - 4+ can include parental controls, age assurance, user content, messaging, advertising and infrequent contests.
  - 13+ includes frequent contests and social media.
  - 16+ includes unrestricted web access.
  - 18+ includes gambling. — [Apple age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/)
- Apple Guideline 2.3.6: answer rating questions honestly and meet local content-rating requirements. Guideline 5.1.4: handle children's data with care and comply with applicable law; a parental gate is not parental consent. Apps outside the Kids category may not imply children are the main audience — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Apple Guidelines 5.2.1 and 5.2.2: no third-party trademarks or copyrighted content without permission, and no misleading names. Guideline 4.2.2: apps should not be primarily marketing, aggregators or link collections — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Google Play Families policy:
  - declare target age groups accurately;
  - apps including children cannot send persistent identifiers such as the advertising ID from children or users of unknown age;
  - no personalised, interest-based or remarketing ads to these users;
  - only self-certified ad SDKs;
  - mixed-audience apps need a neutral age screen or must stop non-approved SDKs collecting children's data;
  - apps aimed solely at children cannot request precise location. — [Google Families](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)

### Inferences
- Seat bookings, food and venue passes redeemed in person should qualify as physical services. A Plus subscription that unlocks in-app features would likely need in-app purchase, while a membership delivering discounts at venues is less clear. Test this before committing to a plan.
- A pure booking app likely rates 4+ on Apple from these definitions, though Apple generates the rating from the questionnaire. Leaderboards and frequent tournaments could move it to 13+, and anything involving wagering pushes it toward 18+ and into section 1.
- If you do not list children as a Play target audience and adopt the adult-account design in section 4, you avoid most of the Families restrictions. Declare this accurately, since Google reviews imagery and terminology too.
- Avoid game logos in screenshots for the same reason as section 8.
- Build enough app-native function (live slots, tickets, reviews) that the app does not look like a thin listing under 4.2.2.

### Gaps
- Live versions of both stores' policies should be checked at submission, because the pages opened show no effective date.
- No Indian law requirement for app age labels was researched.
- Google's IARC questionnaire for this category was not reviewed.

## 10. Trademark: God of Gaming alongside Sony's God of War

### Takeaway
There are two questions for a trademark lawyer: whether a God of X name in gaming invites a confusion or reputation-based objection from Sony, and whether the word God draws an objection on religious grounds. I could not check the registers, so this is issue-spotting only.

### Cited Findings
- Trade Marks Act 1999, via WIPO Lex (read 3 Nov 2026; may not reflect later amendments) — [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/128107):
  - s.11(1): refuses marks similar to an earlier mark for identical or similar goods or services where there is a likelihood of confusion, including of association.
  - s.11(2): refuses similar marks even on dissimilar goods or services if the earlier mark is well known in India and the use would take unfair advantage or cause detriment without due cause.
  - s.29(4): makes similar use on dissimilar goods or services an infringement where the registered mark has a reputation in India and use is unfair.
  - s.29(5): treats use of a registered mark as a trade or business name for such goods or services as infringement.
  - s.9(2)(b): bars registration of a mark containing matter likely to offend the religious feelings of any class of citizens.
- Apple Guideline 5.2.1 bars misleading or copycat names and unlicensed third-party marks in app metadata, so a store complaint is a practical route alongside court action — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Shared wording: God of is the distinctive lead in both names, and both sit in video gaming. A service for booking gaming venues is not a game, but it is close enough for an association argument. Sony could also rely on reputation-based grounds, since God of War is a famous game series. The word Gaming is descriptive, so God of carries the weight of the comparison.
- A defence would stress that the phrase has an ordinary laudatory meaning and that the service differs. Whether that would succeed is a legal judgement I cannot make.
- Religious ground: an examiner or a complainant could raise s.9(2)(b) because of the word God. I have no evidence of how this has been applied to similar marks.
- If the name matters to the founder, clearance is worth doing before spending on branding, app store listings and the domain. Fallback names should be shortlisted at the same time, and logo styling should avoid any resemblance to Sony's series branding.

### Gaps
- No search of the Indian Trade Marks Registry was done (this task allowed public page reading only, with no form submissions), and I have not confirmed Sony's Indian registrations, classes or owner entity. Whether God of War has been declared well known in India is also unconfirmed.
- No search for existing users of God of Gaming or similar names was run because the search budget ended. Existing channels or businesses under that name may exist, and could matter as much as Sony.
- No Indian case law on God-formative marks or s.9(2)(b) in comparable settings was reviewed.
