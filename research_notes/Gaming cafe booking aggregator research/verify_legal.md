## L1. E-Commerce Amendment Rules 2026

**Verdict: confirmed with correction. The gazette exists and I read it.**

Primary source: Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II, Section 3(i), No. 721, Wednesday 9 September 2026. Department of Consumer Affairs, G.S.R. 789(E), F. No. J-10/3/2018-CPU. Rule 1 sets commencement at 1 January 2027. The file stamp is CG-DL-E-10092026-276125 and the digital signature is dated 10 Sep 2026, so the masthead and e-publication dates differ by a day. The issue number, G.S.R. number and commencement date digits do not extract as text from this PDF, so I rendered those lines and read them directly.

All six listed duties are in the gazette text:
- r.4(15): comply with the 2023 Guidelines, self-audit yearly, display a certificate prominently.
- r.5(3)(f): marketplace explains main ranking parameters, now in descending order, with relative importance.
- r.4(12): sponsored listings distinctly identified.
- r.4(13): an announced price reduction shows the prior price, meaning the lowest price in the 30 days before.
- r.4(5): 48-hour acknowledgement, copy of the recorded complaint, redress within one month.
- r.4(7): every entity becomes a partner in National Consumer Helpline convergence.

Correction, from comparing the 2020 gazette (G.S.R. 462(E)):
- The 48-hour acknowledgement and one-month redress were already in 2020 r.4(5). Only the complaint copy is new. This duty applies now, not from 2027.
- The ranking explanation was already in 2020 r.5(3)(f). The amendment adds descending order and widens the definition of ranking.
- Helpline convergence was best-effort in 2020 r.4(7) and is now mandatory.
- Genuinely new: sponsored labels, prior price, self-audit and certificate, seller name on invoices at the same font size, no misleading search manipulation, the bundled-fee bar in r.5(7), consumer-data limits in r.5(6), and seller identifiers in r.6(5)(j).

Scope (2020 r.2): goods and services, marketplace and inventory models, with no size threshold. Only a natural person acting in a personal capacity outside regular commercial activity is excluded. Sellers on a marketplace are outside the definition of e-commerce entity. This closes the report's gap on thresholds.

No corrigendum or deferral turned up in a search on 3 Nov 2026. That is a weak negative.

## L2. Online Gaming Act 2025 and Rules 2026

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

Facts confirmed:
- Rules are G.S.R. 303(E), Gazette of 22 Apr 2026, in force 1 May 2026 (gazette read). Shardul Amarchand (11 May 2026) and King Stubb & Kasiva both give 1 May 2026 for the Act. I did not open the Act's own commencement notification.
- Definitions match in TaxGuru's reproduction and PRS's Bill text: s.2(1)(f) online game, s.2(1)(g) online money game, s.2(1)(i) online social game with access fees allowed if not a stake.
- s.5 covers offering, aiding, abetting and inducing. s.7 covers facilitating payments. s.6 covers advertising. s.10 makes s.5 and s.7 cognizable and non-bailable, and does not list s.6.
- SC Observer shows T.C.(C) 133/2025 pending, but that page was last updated 2 Jul 2026. A PTI report of 5 Aug 2026 says the Court agreed to a final hearing and told parties to complete pleadings. No stay and no date were reported. I found nothing later.
- Three searches found no source on LAN cafes or offline tournaments. Weak negative.

Answer to the question: fair as a reading of the text, not settled. No source confirms it.
- Seat time: a fee for time at a machine carries no expectation of winning money. s.2(1)(i) and r.9(c)(ii) both treat access fees as separate from stakes. That supports the reading.
- But outside the Act overstates it. An online title played at a cafe is still an online game. r.2(1)(h) defines an online game service provider to include anyone who offers, organises or makes available online games, which could reach a venue or platform. Registration duties for social games apply only if the Centre notifies them. s.16 lets authorised officers enter any place, physical or digital, and search and arrest without warrant.
- Tournaments: a paid-entry event with a prize fits s.2(1)(g) unless the game is a registered e-sport. But r.9(c)(i) lists entry fees, administrative costs and performance prizes as a separate category from bets and stakes in r.9(c)(iii). So it is a weighed factor, not an automatic ban. A prize pool funded by entry fees looks most exposed.
- Purely local play with no communication technology may not be an online game at all. LAN is unclear.

## L3. DPDP

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- Gazette No. 760 (G.S.R. 846(E), Rules) and No. 757 (G.S.R. 843(E), commencement) carry the masthead Thursday 13 Nov 2025 and file stamps of 14112025. PIB's 17 Nov 2025 note says 14 Nov. Shardul Amarchand uses 14 Nov. So the two-date framing is right.
- Eighteen months gives 13 or 14 May 2027 (recomputed). Rule 1(4) and the commencement notice both say eighteen months, and the latter covers ss.7 to 10.
- dpdprules.org reports a PIB release of 16 May 2026 quoting the MeitY Secretary on 13 May 2027, and treats it as reported speech. I did not open that release.
- s.9(1) and s.9(3) confirmed. Rule 10 confirmed in the gazette.
- Fourth Schedule confirmed in the gazette. Part A: health, education, creche and child transport. Part B: legal functions, public benefits, email-only accounts, real-time location for safety, blocking harmful content, and confirming a user is not a child. No booking or entertainment class. Part B is by purpose, so any fiduciary can use it, but it does not cover ordinary bookings.

Correction: PIB says the highest penalty is Rs 250 Cr for security-safeguard failure, Rs 200 Cr each for breach notification and children's obligations, and Rs 50 Cr for other violations. So penalties up to Rs 200 Cr is right only for children's data.

Update: a Removal of Difficulties Order, S.O. 5458(E), early October 2026, inserts a preposition in s.9(1). No change of substance or date. India Briefing gives 13 March 2027 for ss.9 and 10, which looks like a slip, since the gazette's eighteen months gives May 2027.

## L4. RBI payment aggregator Direction

**Verdict: confirmed.**

RBI page opened, dated 15 Sep 2025 (RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141), with no amendment marked. Para 4(f) merchant includes a marketplace. Para 10(b) bars a PA business from marketplace business. Para 6 sets Rs 15 Cr at application and Rs 25 Cr by the end of the third financial year of authorisation, maintained after that. Para 13(f) limits an onboarded marketplace to accepting payments for sellers onboarded on its platform. Para 13(g) requires funds due to a merchant to reach that merchant's account.

The Direction does not say outright that a marketplace collecting for sellers needs no authorisation. MediaNama's phrasing of Rs 25 Cr for the next three years is loose. Use RBI's wording.

## L5. RBI prepaid instruments Direction

**Verdict: confirmed.**

RBI page opened, headed updated as on 30 Sep 2026. Para 2.1: closed-system instruments are for purchases from the issuer only, cannot pay for third-party services, and sit outside RBI authorisation. Paras 2.8 and 9.1: Small PPIs work at a group of identified merchants with a specific contract. Para 4.1: non-banks need authorisation. Para 4.5: Rs 5 Cr at application, Rs 15 Cr by the end of the third financial year after final authorisation.

The inference is sound and now has better support. RBI's draft Direction of 22 Apr 2026, para 4.1(f) Explanation (ii), excludes an entity that is a marketplace from the closed-system treatment. AZB and Cyril Amarchand read it the same way. Draft net worth is unchanged at Rs 5 Cr and Rs 15 Cr. I found no final version, and the page shows no 2026 circular explaining the 30 Sep update.

## L6. Razorpay Route and Cashfree Easy Split

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- Route: linked accounts, transfers from payments or balance, and holds with no documented maximum. A refund debits the main balance.
- Correction: reversal need not be a separate step. reverse_all recovers the transfer for a full refund, or a partial refund on a payment sent to one account. The separate reversal API is needed for a partial refund on a payment sent to several accounts.
- Missed fact: Razorpay's docs apply RBI's Sept 2025 Direction as a gate. The business itself needs domestic turnover above Rs 40 lakh (GST-3B) in the current or preceding year, or export turnover above Rs 5 lakh, and each linked account must deal directly with customers. This traces to Table 1 item (iv) of the RBI Direction.
- Cashfree: FAQ confirms up to 45 days from the transaction date, not extendable. With no action, the held amount settles to the merchant, not the vendor. The FAQ does not mention the Rs 40 lakh test.

## L7. GST

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- TCS: Notification 15/2024-Central Tax, dated 10 Jul 2024 (official copy read), changes the CGST rate to 0.25%. The combined rate is 0.5%, or 0.5% IGST.
- Registration regardless of turnover under s.24(x): per Masters India. I did not open the Act.
- s.9(5): recreation is not listed. Masters India's list is out of date: Notification 17/2025-CT(R) added local delivery from 22 Sep 2025 (read).
- 28% ruling: Bandai Namco (Maharashtra AAR, 8 Apr 2019; upheld on appeal). TheTaxTalk and Amusements & Attractions show it turned on access to amusement facilities in entry (iiia). TaxGuru's free text does not show the entry wording, so the cited page alone does not prove this. GST Gyaan shows the earlier wording.
- Notification 6/2021-CT(R) (official copy on J&K's tax site) replaced items (iii) and (iiia) from 1 Oct 2021. Item (iiia) now covers casinos, race clubs and IPL-type events only.
- Better support the report lacks: CBIC Circular 164/20/2021-GST, para 10 (TaxGuru reproduction). The 18% entry covers places with rides etc, indoor or outdoor, unless a casino or race club is accessible. The clarification also applies to the pre-amendment entries. Grant Thornton questions a circular's legal force, per a search result only.
- Notification 15/2025-CT(R) changed items (ii), (iiia) and (iv) and leaves (iii) and (vi) alone (read). So 18% stands.
- A PC or console cafe is not named in item (iii), so residual item (vi) may be the home. Both are 18%. No ruling on a gaming cafe was found.

## L9. Rajkot

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- High Court order of 13 Jun 2024 (read): TRP ran from about June 2021 with only a police permission under s.33(x), which covers ticket sales, and no s.33(w) licence. The first statement is prima facie and a later paragraph treats it as evident from the Corporation's note. No building use permission. Structural and fire NOC queries came only in May 2024.
- The 101 figure and eight cities come from one report (Asianet Newsable, 29 May 2024, unnamed urban development department sources): all 101 registered facilities inspected, none allowed to operate, 20 sealed, 81 temporarily closed. Free Press Journal confirms a statewide closure order on 26 May 2024 but gives no count. LiveLaw reports a halt with no count. The order gives no such number.
- Inference: TRP shows a licence of the wrong class is not proof of safety. But the order says the structure was used without adhering to fire norms, with the fire NOC still being queried in May 2024, so TRP is no example of a NOC failing. The fire NOC half rests on Ahmedabad. There, Gujarat Samachar (30 May 2024) says action was taken against units with a NOC but no working systems. No count, and the inspection covered hospitals and other properties too.

## L10. Game licences

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- PSN Terms v12 (April 2026) s.24.9 and s.18.2; Software Usage Terms s.1.4, 1.4(a), 1.4(g). Confirmed.
- Microsoft, published 30 Jul 2026, effective 30 Sep 2026: s.13.a.i says personal and non-commercial. The word non-business is not there, in either part of the page I read. s.13.i covers digital goods and public performance.
- Riot (1 Dec 2024): s.1.1, s.3.1, s.7.1 rule 17 (business use needs express written consent), s.14.1(2). Confirmed.
- Steam (revised 10 Sep 2026): s.2.G(iii) bars commercial exploitation unless expressly permitted in the agreement or subscription terms. Written consent is the route in s.2.G(i) for rent, lease or licence. So bars commercial use without consent is imprecise.
- PC Cafe: Valve calls it the official way to run Steam in a cafe or public venue. Opted-in titles only, no offline mode. India is not named. Non-US corporations and sole proprietors are accepted. Non-US partnerships taxed at partner level are not.
- Only formal route confirmed holds as a limited-search result. Riot ran an activation with 12 Indian cafes in 2023 (InsideSport), but no licence scheme is reported. I found nothing for Sony or Microsoft. Absence only.

## L11. App stores

**Verdict: confirmed.**

Apple 3.1.3(e) says goods or services consumed outside the app must use methods other than in-app purchase. Google Play policy section 3 says its billing must not be used for physical services, listing gym memberships, airfare and live event tickets. Neither names venue bookings, so applying them is a small inference. Digital features unlocked in the app still need store billing under Apple 3.1.1 and Google section 2.

## L12. CCPA drip pricing

**Verdict: confirmed with correction.**

- Timing is wrong. FirstCry's Rs 2 lakh penalty was reported 26 to 29 Sep 2025 (MediaNama, Policy Edge). Zepto's Rs 7 lakh was reported 5 to 6 Dec 2025 (Inc42, MediaNama), after a show-cause notice of 30 Jan 2025. August 2026 is when a Rajya Sabha reply listed them and gave about Rs 20 lakh since the June 2025 advisory.
- Listed amounts add up: 7 + 5 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 20. But that needs SpiceJet at Rs 1 lakh, while New Kerala lists SpiceJet as corrective action only. Sources conflict.
- All-in pricing is a sound inference but not a stated rule.
  - For it: AZB (6 Dec 2023) says charges common to most users should be in the displayed price and names convenience fees among the targets. Zepto was told to disclose all charges upfront, and CCPA rejected its argument that sellers levied the fee, because the platform controls the display (MediaNama, 30 Dec 2025). 2020 rules 6(5)(b) and 7(1)(e) require a total price in a single figure with a breakup.
  - Against reading it as settled: the FirstCry direction, as reported, requires tax-inclusive prices with extras such as shipping disclosed. The Guidelines' basket-sneaking proviso excludes necessary fees disclosed at purchase. No opened order names a convenience fee. Annexure illustrations are guidance only.

## New findings the claims miss

1. Route's Rs 40 lakh turnover gate applies to the platform itself. A pre-revenue startup may not qualify. My inference: if GST-3B shows only commission and fees, then at a 10% take rate Rs 40 lakh of fees means about Rs 4 Cr of bookings. Ask Razorpay and counsel.
2. RBI's April 2026 draft expressly denies the closed-system route to marketplace-issued instruments.
3. 2020 rule 4(8): no cancellation charge on consumers unless the entity bears similar charges when it cancels. Not touched by the 2026 amendment. The report lists this as an unconfirmed gap. Rule 6(4) also requires each seller to have a prior written contract and its own grievance officer, which means every cafe.
4. Online Gaming Rules r.2(1)(h) and Act s.16 reach further towards venues than the report suggests.
5. CBIC Circular 164/20/2021-GST is the best authority for 18%.
