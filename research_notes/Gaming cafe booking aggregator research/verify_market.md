## M1. City counts and the trustworthiness of the method

**Seen, Pune (last updated 17 Oct 2026; screen 16 Oct):** 19 Google Maps searches, 290 places opened, 103 kept (open, PIN 411 or 412, at least 10 Google reviews, PC or console; arcades, VR and sim centres, pool halls, board game cafes and shops out). 59 also confirmed by a second source such as own website or Instagram. The other 44 look genuine but have no site to check and are not named. Only the 59 are listed. No funnel from 290 to 103, no queries, no file.

**Seen, Gurugram (screen 12 Oct, recheck 16 Oct):** 409 listings, 6 kept as the ones confirmed open, same 10-review bar, PIN 122. No funnel and no unconfirmed tier. Relies on each cafe's own photos, posts or site. Reviews of the six range from 10 to 420. The page says an earlier version listed cafes and photos it could not verify, and that version was replaced.

**In the method's favour:**
- Method is dated and disclosed, and the 59 can be sample-audited through Maps links.
- It admits no visits and ratings are Google's.
- Chain outlets match the chains' own sites: ISST 6 of 6, Intencity 3 of 3, Refill24x7 2 of 2, Rapid Rounds Pune and Undri.
- The advertise page sells sponsored articles, reviews and brand partnerships to 25,000+ monthly pageviews. It does not offer paid cafe inclusion, though it does not rule it out either. The featured cafe is labelled unpaid.

**Against:**
- Publisher earns affiliate commission; its editor says he works as an SEO partner for startups. An owner-verified badge is offered with no fee terms stated.
- One author, one day, no visits. Open means open on Google Maps. The 103 cannot be audited. The 10-review floor drops new and micro cafes. 22 of the 59 have under 50 reviews.
- Same site, conflicting claims: Pune 10+ on its national guide against 103 here; Bangalore, Pune and Delhi are each called the largest on different pages; the Bangalore guide claims personal verification while the Pune guide says no visits.
- Its Delhi guide says Gurugram has cafes around Cyber Hub, Udyog Vihar and DLF Phase, while none of the six is in those areas. Search snippets still show older text naming Gurugram venues that are not among the six.
- 11 other city guides I opened (Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi, Hyderabad, Noida, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Nagpur, Indore, Lucknow) have no screen. Jaipur and Bhopal were not checked for one.

**Like-for-like [calc]:** Gurugram's 6 requires own-source confirmation, so it compares with Pune's 59, not 103. On 2011 census populations (Pune UA 5,057,709; Gurugram city 876,969), Pune is 20 per million on 103 and 12 on 59; Gurugram is 7. The gap is about 1.7 times, not 17 times. Pass rates differ (6 of 409 = 1.5%; 103 of 290 = 36%) and cannot be explained without the funnel.

**Rating:** medium-low. Good enough for an order-of-magnitude floor, not for planning. A Google Places sweep of both cities would settle it.

## M2. National counts and the 2,000 estimate

**Seen:**
- Franchise India, 7 Jan 2019: nearly 100 gaming cafes plus under 1,000 non-exclusive. Not attributed. The claim leaves out the "nearly 100".
- Whatifbelief, undated: over 15,000 establishments across major cities, no source.
- Gamingnear listings: no national count and no 15,000. 332 results, some in the US, Canada and Australia.
- The 300 to 350 (2017) figure is on none of the three cited pages. iTechPost (16 Sep 2017) says about 300. NVIDIA's white paper is too large for the fetch tool, so 350 stays unopened.
- Extra: the CII-linked Indian Digital Gaming Society page says over 300 esports cafes serving over 40,000 gamers, undated with a 2026 footer and FY2022 market data. It is a trade-body number, but plainly old.
- Arithmetic [calc]: 1,020, 2,000, 3,640. Correct.

**Stress test [calc], using Census 2011:** 53 million-plus UAs (52 final), 468 Class I, urban population about 377M. Pune is 1.34% of urban India.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Base 2,000 | 5.3 per million urban, needs Pune to over-index 3.8x on 103, or 2.2x on 59 |
| Low 1,020 | Pune would hold 10% of India's cafes, a 7.5x over-index: hard to believe |
| High 3,640 | 2.1x over-index on 103, or 1.2x on 59 |
| Urban India at Gurugram's density | 2,580 |
| Urban India at Pune's 59 density | 4,400 |
| Urban India at Pune's 103 density | 7,680 (ceiling, since small towns will be thinner) |

- **Flat metros are the main structural flaw.** Pune is the smallest of the eight metros on 2011 population yet sets the base of 100 for all. 8 metros (85M) at 7, 12 and 20 per million give 582, 992 and 1,732, against the model's 400, 800 and 1,440.
- **Tier 2 looks fair.** About 45 UAs (75.6M, average 1.68M) against 40 assumed. 8, 15 and 25 per city is about 5, 9 and 15 per million.
- **Towns:** 415 other Class I towns against 150 assumed. Base of 600 is about 2.8 per million across all remaining urban India, which is plausible, so this tier is less clearly wrong.
- **Possible double discount:** the model then cuts by 55% for reachability. If the universe is already close to the confirmed tier, that discounts twice.

**Independent anchors:** none directly counts cafes today. All are floors or other definitions.
- Under 1,100 total in January 2019; 2,000 needs about 2x in seven years. Fits, but the anchor is unattributed.
- GameBiller: 160+ Indian PS5 shops with at least ten sessions; 1,700+ centres worldwide at 2.9 stations each, so sign-ups rather than cafes.
- Gujarat: 101 registered entertainment facilities in eight cities, May 2024, which are game zones generally.
- xMap: 6,334 amusement parks and arcades, March 2025, no cafe category.
- App networks: Console 34, Respawna 22.
- Poidata's 24,709 game stores refused my fetch.

**View:** range looks too low, not too high, especially at the bottom. With Pune over-indexing 2x to 4x (my judgement), about 1,900 to 3,800 cafes with 10+ reviews, and about 1,100 to 2,200 digitally confirmable. So 2,000 fits the confirmable tier better than the all-cafe tier. This rests on one city from one affiliate site. The 15,000 stays unsupported, though a count including micro-parlours could be well above 3,600; no data exists to size it.

## M3. List prices

**Seen, GameBiller (17 Aug 2026, updated 26 Oct 2026):** live rate cards of Indian cafes on its platform with at least ten sessions; one player, one hour, weekday, middle half of shops.
- PC ₹80 to 120, most often ₹100. PS5 ₹100 to 160, most often ₹120.
- The 160+ count is stated for PS5 only. No PC sample size is given.
- Group console multiples 1.8, 2.5 and 3.2 for 2, 3 and 4 players.

**Seen, CafeNex (18 Aug 2026):** metro mid-range PC ₹50 to 80, tier-2 ₹30 to 40, premium ₹80 to 120, console ₹150 to 300 per station, not per controller. No data behind any of these.

**Note [calc]:** at ₹120 solo, GameBiller implies ₹216 for two and ₹384 for four, so the console figures are not really in conflict. The PC figures do conflict. Pune cafe sites span ₹50 to ₹130.

## M4. Prepaid packs

**Seen:**
- Refill24x7: 20 hours ₹800, 165Hz only, ₹40 an hour against ₹60 list.
- Intencity: ₹62.50 an hour on 100 hours appears only in its FAQ; no pack price shown; ₹6,250 is implied [calc]; tier probably 240Hz. Listed packs: 10 hours ₹900, 50 hours ₹3,500.
- Gamerz Nation: 100 hours ₹8,500, ₹85 an hour, against ₹120 list.

**Correction:** these are pack list prices at three Pune cafes. Nothing shows the share of hours sold on packs, or unused hours. "Realised" is therefore an inference.
- The ₹90 top comes from shorter packs.
- Time-limited offers go lower: Refill day pass ₹35, Early Bird ₹30 [calc]; Gamerz Nation morning ₹62.50.
- CafeNex's own counters (₹4.8M over 34k hours) give ₹141 per billed hour [calc], possibly with food and consoles. So the realised average is not established.

## M5. Occupancy

**Seen:**
- CafeNex: 25% to 35% typical, 45%+ excellent, peaks 70% to 90%, weekday mornings near zero, break-even within 6 to 12 months at 35%+.
- GameBiller: very few floors above 40% across all open hours, with no data shown.
- IZI: 25% to 30% launch, 45% to 55% base after 3 to 6 months, 40% to 50% weighted, peaks 85% to 90%, weekday afternoons 15% to 20%, break-even 35% to 50%, all illustrative.

**Corrections:**
- IZI conflicts with the rest; it does not support them.
- "All modelled" is not quite right: GameBiller holds billing data and may have measured, but it does not show this.
- "Healthy" is the wrong word: CafeNex ties break-even to 35%+ and Hash has 30% barely covering costs. Use "typical".

**Cross-check [calc]:** Pune listing ₹80,000 a month on 10 PCs at ₹70 to ₹100 is 22% to 32%. Bengaluru ₹45,000 on 3 PS5s at ₹120 to ₹150 is 28% to 35%. Both assume 360 open hours and my rate guesses. This supports the lower band.

## M7. Revenue model

| Case | Bookings a year | GMV | Commission | SaaS | Total | SaaS share | Per booking | Per cafe a year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 27,375 | ₹54.75 lakh | ₹2.74 lakh | ₹4.49 lakh | ₹7.23 lakh | 62% | ₹10 | ₹4,819 |
| Base | 2,92,000 | ₹10.22 Cr | ₹1.022 Cr | ₹23.98 lakh | ₹1.262 Cr | 19% | ₹35 | ₹31,544 |
| High | 18,25,000 | ₹109.5 Cr | ₹16.43 Cr | ₹1.68 Cr | ₹18.10 Cr | 9% | ₹90 | ₹1,81,042 |

₹10 Cr at ₹35: 28.6 lakh bookings, 7,828 a day, 19.6 per cafe at 400. Correct.

**Sensitivity, one input at a time around base (₹1.26 Cr):**

| Input | Swing | Result | Width |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cafes | 150 to 1,000 | ₹0.47 to 3.15 Cr | ₹2.68 Cr |
| Bookings per cafe per day | 0.5 to 5 | ₹0.50 to 2.80 Cr | ₹2.30 Cr |
| AOV | ₹200 to 600 | ₹0.82 to 1.99 Cr | ₹1.17 Cr |
| Take rate | 5% to 15% | ₹0.75 to 1.77 Cr | ₹1.02 Cr |
| SaaS price | ₹499 to 1,999 | ₹1.14 to 1.50 Cr | ₹0.36 Cr |
| SaaS attach | 50% to 70% | ₹1.26 to 1.36 Cr | ₹0.10 Cr |

Cafes and bookings per cafe move it most, and bookings has the least evidence. If AOV and take are treated as one input (₹10, ₹35, ₹90 per booking), the swing is ₹0.53 to 2.87 Cr, as large as bookings.

**Playo check:**
- 600,000 games (28 May 2026 release; "games" undefined, 100+ cities) over 5,000+ venue partners (29 Apr 2026 release, 150+ cities, includes abroad) is 120 a month, 4 a day. Correct.
- Hudle: 150,000+ over 2,000+ (Jul 2025) is 2.5 a day.
- So 2 a day is half Playo and 80% of Hudle, in categories where a court needs a reservation. Cafes are mostly walk-in. Not conservative.

**Revenue check (FY25 ₹20.5 Cr, Inc42; periods do not match, order of magnitude only):**
- Playo earns about ₹41,000 a venue and about ₹28 a game (₹39 if a 37% FY26 rise is applied).
- Base is 77% of Playo per venue and about the same per booking.
- High is 4.4x per venue and 2.3x to 3.2x per booking, and its total nearly equals all of Playo's FY25 revenue from a fifth of the venues.

**Structure:**
1. The four commission inputs move together, so the commission spread is 600x and the total spread 250x.
2. They should be negatively linked: a 15% take and 70% SaaS attach sit beside GameBiller (commission-free, bookings in its ₹499 plan, free tier to 5 stations) and Clutchr (0%, from ₹799).
3. The business changes type: Low is mostly SaaS.
4. Physical limit, from the model's own AOV build-up (2.6, 4.25, 7.5 seat-hours). A 15-seat cafe at 30% over 12 hours bills 54 seat-hours. Low takes 2%, base 16%, high 69%, and high is 104% at 10 seats. Respawna's 8.4 stations per cafe makes base about 28%. Against 10 to 30 visits a day, base is 11% to 34%, high 42% to 125%. The ₹10 Cr case needs 85 seat-hours a day.
5. Missing: ramp, churn, closures, no-shows, leakage, seasonality, gateway costs, and the cost to sign and retain cafes.
6. AOV looks high: listings give ₹100 to ₹267 per visit at PC or PS5 shops, and Console's graphic shows ₹170 online (unverified, may be illustrative). At ₹250, base falls to ₹97 lakh; with 1 booking a day as well, ₹60.5 lakh.

## M11. Hash model (published 31 Oct 2026)

**Seen:** 10 PCs and 2 PS5s, tier 2, 14 hours, 30 days, ₹70, ₹24.5 lakh setup, no line items.

| Occupancy | Revenue | Profit | Margin | Payback | 10% fee as share of profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30% | ₹1.15 lakh | ₹9,600 | not stated | over 20 years | 120% |
| 45% | ₹1.72 lakh | ₹64,000 | 37% | 39 months | 27% |
| 60% | ₹2.29 lakh | ₹1.19 lakh | 52% | 21 months | 19% |

**Recomputed:**
- 10% of ₹1.15 lakh is ₹11,500. Correct.
- 5,040 station-hours at ₹70 gives ₹1,05,840, ₹1,58,760, ₹2,11,680, not the article's figures.
- Stated figures and its ₹57,000 per 15 points all imply about ₹76 an hour. A PS5 rate near ₹105 would fit exactly (my inference; the article does not say so).
- On literal inputs and the same costs, profit at 30% is about ₹440 and 10% is ₹10,584.
- Payback checks out: 21.3 years, 38 months, 20.6 months.
- Implied costs ₹1.05 to 1.10 lakh, so variable cost is about 4% of revenue.

**Holding together:**
- ₹2 lakh a seat is above CafeNex's ₹0.75 to 1.67 lakh.
- ₹70 is below GameBiller's ₹100.
- 14 hours differs from the 12 used elsewhere.
- Hardware replacement and owner pay are not visibly included.

**Flat 10% is a stress case:** only app bookings pay. At 15% of revenue via the app and 30% occupancy the fee is ₹1,725, or 18% of profit. Incremental bookings keep about 96% before commission, so the real fight is over walk-ins that switch to the app.

## M12. Peak and quiet hours

**Seen:**
- GameBiller (17 Aug 2026): advance booking turns a queue into a schedule; book only hours you actually fill, as quiet-hour reservations block walk-ins; discounting a full Saturday adds nothing. It treats peak demand as given. It does not say peak seats need no demand.
- Clutchr (4 Jul 2026): recommends 30% to 40% off for weekdays 11am to 5pm; does not mention 50%.
- GameBiller's pricing post suggests lower weekday afternoon rates, no percentage, and no share of cafes using them.

**Rate cards [calc]:**
- Intencity 30% to 31% off, Mon to Fri 8am to 4pm.
- Gamerz Nation 48% off, 8am to 12pm.
- Refill 50% off via its 2 plus 2 hour Early Bird and Night Hawk.
- A Jaipur cafe up to 50% via magicpin (Gaming Nation).

So 30% to 50% exists on some cards, but "already discounted" is not shown as the norm.

**Inference check:** CafeNex's 70% to 90% peak leaves 10% to 30% empty, and it treats 80%+ weekend peaks plus waitlists as an expansion signal, so not all cafes reach that. Reserving at peak is a queue tool, so a player-side fee for guaranteed seats survives this argument. A demand-based commission at peak does not.
