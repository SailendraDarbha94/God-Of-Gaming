## 1. What do adjacent models teach about cold start, fragmented low-tech supply, leakage and fees?

### Takeaway
Three patterns recur. Winners start in a tight geography or niche, they own the venue's workflow or the payment, and the newest pricing charges a venue only for genuinely new customers, leaving repeat visits free. Indian going-out platforms earn about 9% of transaction value, while a high-touch managed model such as Urban Company earns about 26%. Nobody publishes a measured leakage rate.

#### Cold-start tactics with a precedent
- 16 of 17 marketplaces interviewed began with a constraint, usually geographic for local services. OpenTable's rule of thumb was 50 to 100 concentrated restaurants in a city, and Uber launch teams aimed for 30+ drivers and pickups under 15 minutes — [Rachitsky, 20 Nov 2019](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace) STALE [practitioner]
- NFX lists software for one side (OpenTable, Honeybook, StyleSeat), making supply look bigger by aggregating web data (Yelp, Indeed), connecting sides by hand (Zappos), a geographic constraint (Lyft), subsidising the key side (Uber, ClassPass), meetups (Poshmark, Yelp) and asking only one side to change behaviour (Square). It warns against fake activity — [NFX, Jul 2019](https://www.nfx.com/post/19-marketplace-tactics-for-overcoming-the-chicken-or-egg-problem) STALE [practitioner]
- Gurley's checklist: fragmentation helps, supplier sign-up friction hurts (OpenTable's need for a PC and internet slowed city launches), aggregating demand is harder than aggregating supply, frequency matters, verticals where one good match ends the search are weak, and being in the payment flow turns the platform from a tax into a partner. OpenTable's reservations per restaurant rose as penetration rose — [Gurley, 13 Nov 2012](https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/) STALE [practitioner]
- Single-player tools as kindling for a network, using Delicious and Instagram as examples — [Dixon, 31 Jan 2015](https://cdixon.org/2015/01/31/come-for-the-tool-stay-for-the-network/) STALE [practitioner]
- ClassPass went from Classivity in 2010 to Passport in 2012, offering one class at a new studio, then a subscription and the ClassPass name in 2013-14, added cafes as credit partners in 2024, and was acquired by Mindbody in October 2021 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClassPass). The page does not explain why each step happened, and I could not open a primary account.
- Justdial shows how few directory listings become payers: 567,000 active paid campaigns against 41.6M listings, about 1.4% [my calc], with 166M users in the December 2023 quarter — [Jan 2024 call](https://images.jdmagicbox.com/investors/justdial-earnings-call-transcript-january-15-2024-240117062459.pdf) STALE [filing]. Q2 FY27 revenue was ₹342.8 Cr and net profit ₹107.8 Cr, per an announcement summarised at [Screener](https://www.screener.in/company/JUSTDIAL/consolidated/).

#### Leakage
- Urban Company's RHP lists circumvention as a named risk factor: people may meet on the platform and then deal directly to avoid fees. The one measure it names is barring cancellation of a service order after the service has started. It says it is not aware of material or large-scale circumvention for FY23 to FY25 and the June 2025 quarter. Its commissioned Redseer section says weaker earnings or a weaker quality guarantee would push both sides offline. Cohort NTV stays high: the FY24 cohort spent 0.77 times its first-year value in FY25, and the FY18 cohort reached 1.75 times by FY25 — [RHP, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/filings/public-issues/sep-2025/urban-company-limited-rhp_96511.html) [filing; the issuer has a motive to play this down]
- Airbnb bans pre-booking contact off-platform, links that steer users away, discounts for booking off-platform and rebooking off-platform. It ties this to protections that only work on the platform, and enforces with warnings up to account removal — [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799) [official], © 2026. That works for a stranger booking once; it maps poorly to a neighbourhood cafe.
- New-client-only pricing removes most of the motive to leak: see Treatwell, Mindbody, Booksy and Fresha above.
- Eternal frames the same risk for agents: where the product exists apart from the platform, an agent can route around it, so District sits in the medium-to-high risk zone. A durable position comes from physical operations, or in the letter's phrase "where infrastructure becomes the product" — [Q2 FY27 letter](https://b.zmtcdn.com/investor-relations/Eternal_Limited_Shareholders_Letter_Q2FY27_Results.pdf) [filing]
- Sports-booking signals: a four-year Playo user says court booking alone could go direct after the first visit and stays for peer games, and reviewers on every app advise booking direct after a failure — [playo_sentiment.md] [sibling]. Vendors claim over 70% of a turf's volume is repeat teams, with no data — [Clutchr](https://clutchr.in/blog/turf-business-profitability-guide) [vendor] [sibling]

Regulars sit on prepaid packs at ₹40 to ₹90 an hour, so they are the leakage cohort by default — [market_economics.md] [sibling]

### Inferences
- **Benchmarks for a cafe aggregator.** On a ticket as small as ₹170, 20% is ₹34 and 35% is about ₹60 [my calc]. New-client pricing is the right shape but cannot carry the business alone, because each customer pays only once. It needs a second line such as membership, group sales or sponsorship.
- **Gurley's checklist, scored by me for cafes.** Fragmentation strong. Frequency strong for regulars, but regulars leak. Sign-up friction moderate. Payment flow weak today, because cash, UPI and WhatsApp dominate. Network effects weak unless teammates and tournaments are added. One-match risk is real: once a player finds their cafe, search is over.
- **Nine percent is the realistic Indian benchmark.** Swiggy and District reach about 9% with brand, discounts, ads and ticket fees together, and Playo looks similar, so 15% to 20% commission on all bookings looks unrealistic. A higher rate can only be defended on a first booking. This is a judgement across different business mixes.
- **Payments are the shared tactic.** Swiggy's KPI, Gurley's factor and Eternal's dining description all point to being in the transaction at the venue, including for walk-ins.
- **Managed quality earns a higher take but costs more.** Urban Company's 26% comes with training, guarantees and ID enforcement, which a cafe aggregator will not replicate at ₹170 tickets.

## 2. Is a free or cheap venue SaaS a better wedge than a marketplace in fragmented markets?

### Takeaway
The evidence favours a tool that becomes the venue's source of truth, but as a means to reach demand and not a business in itself. The strongest precedent is exact: LANGAME in Russia pairs club software with a player booking app across 4,505 listed clubs. Against this, Indian cafe software is already crowded and nearly free, and software alone has not produced demand for the vendors who tried adding a finder. All evidence is case-based and survivor-biased; I found no controlled comparison.

#### Against, or limits
- Gurley says aggregating demand is the harder and more critical job, and that software requirements slowed OpenTable's rollout — [Gurley, 2012](https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/) STALE.
- The Indian price floor is near zero. GameBiller is free to 5 stations then ₹499 a month; GamingCafe.io ₹799; Clutchr ₹799 to ₹2,999; CafeNex ₹999 to ₹1,499 — [market_economics.md] [sibling]. CafeSynk Lite is free for Indian cafes and Premium is ₹600 per PC monthly; it says both 50+ and 297 cafes on one page — [CafeSynk](https://cafesynk.com/) [company-claimed].
- At ₹499 to ₹2,999 a month, 1,000 cafes yield only ₹60 lakh to ₹3.6 Cr a year [my calc]. This assumes 1,000 paying, about half the sibling's base-case universe of 2,000 cafes.
- Software sign-ups do not equal active floors: GameBiller's claims work out to about 2.9 stations and 76 invoices per centre — [market_economics.md] [sibling].
- A SaaS vendor adding a consumer finder has not shown demand. GameBiller's web finder lists turfs, gaming cafes and kids' play areas and asks only for a mobile number, but gives no cities or venue count — [GameBiller](https://www.gamebiller.com/book). Playzor's headline counters render as zero — [india_competitors.md] [sibling].
- Free tools for small Indian merchants have been hard to monetise, or capital-hungry: Khatabook raised $186.5M with FY24 revenue of ₹113.8 Cr (STALE; Inc42's ₹1.4 Cr for FY25 looks like a different entity) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/khatabook/financials/). Practo, software plus marketplace, had ₹152 Cr revenue and about ₹9.4 Cr profit in FY24, STALE — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/practo/financials/). Inc42 warns its data is as-supplied, and its pages show internal conflicts.
- Demand-side counter-example: FITPASS reached ₹61.7 Cr with a membership and only $2M raised — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/fitpass/financials/). It sells to consumers and corporates, not software to gyms, although I did not establish its corporate share.
- The best cafes already have software. Intencity in Pune runs ggLeap — [Intencity](https://intencity.in). A rival tool must integrate or displace it.

### Inferences
- **My read: the wedge is the floor, not billing.** Do not try to out-feature billing vendors at ₹499. Own what they lack: live station status that a player can trust, plus a demand promise. A free floor tool in a tight launch cluster, in exchange for live inventory, seems the best first offer. Software revenue comes later.
- **Copy LANGAME's shape, then localise.** Directory first, software beneath it, community on top. Its name and package suggest it grew out of a software vendor, though I could not confirm its history.
- **Do not assume Russia transfers.** It has 4,505 listed clubs versus my sibling's estimate of about 2,000 in India, and club culture and prices differ. I found no revenue data for LANGAME.
- **A startup with thin capital should not build all of this at once.** Hash already sells kiosk software and books players, so a floor-control race against an active rival in Mumbai may be expensive. Choosing a different first city avoids that fight.
- **On this evidence, marketplace-only struggles on trust and SaaS-only struggles on demand.** Neither looks enough alone in this market.

### Gaps
- No controlled or cross-sectional study comparing SaaS-first with marketplace-first. No evidence found, not evidence of absence.
- LANGAME's revenue, take rate, booking volume, share of clubs with live booking, and founding story could not be read. Software and business pages refused fetching. A person should read them, and Russian-language press could be searched in a follow-up.
- OpenTable's early adoption numbers and S-1 were not opened.
- I found no list of cafes per software vendor in India apart from company claims.

#### RBI rules on a cross-cafe wallet
- Closed-system prepaid instruments are limited to buying from the issuing entity itself. Instruments usable at a group of merchants under contract with the issuer are Small PPIs, or Full-KYC PPIs, and need RBI authorisation. A non-bank needs ₹5 Cr net worth at application and ₹15 Cr by the end of its third financial year. Minimum validity is a year from last load. Gift PPIs are capped at ₹10,000 and cannot be reloaded. Master Direction dated 27 Aug 2021, last listed update 30 Sep 2026 — [RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12156) [official]

#### Product note from the owner side
- Vendor advice worth testing: charge a small fee so people turn up, register in advance, prize in play credit, judge events by how many attendees return next month — [GameBiller, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.gamebiller.com/blog/running-tournaments-at-your-gaming-cafe) [vendor]. Give a specific wait estimate, and take bookings only for hours that fill — [GameBiller](https://www.gamebiller.com/blog/managing-peak-hours-in-a-gaming-cafe) [vendor]. Publish a stated maximum session length at peak — same post.

### Inferences
- **Ship squad booking before teammate finding.** Group play has the better evidence, needs no third-party approval and is viral by invite. Matching strangers can follow as open seats in a squad.
- **Spec tiers belong in the search filter.** Indian players already pay more for 240Hz, and District's slot format cannot express that.
- **Do not launch a cross-cafe stored balance without counsel or a licensed partner.** A plain reading of the Direction suggests a multi-cafe wallet is a Small PPI that a startup cannot issue alone. Safer first steps: per-venue packs sold on the cafe's behalf, or a monthly membership that buys discounts, with credits that are not stored money. Whether a credit subscription escapes the PPI rules is a question for counsel.
- **Ladders should reward visits, not skill.** Riot bars alternatives to its ranking, and peer scores get gamed. Hours, streaks and venue badges avoid both problems.
- **Tournaments should be run for retention and sponsorship, not fees.** Free entry with sponsor-funded prizes in play credit fits both the vendor advice and the legal caution.

### Gaps
- No measured conversion, retention or booking uplift for station maps, specs, game search, Discord or Steam links, in any market. No evidence found, not evidence of absence.
- I could not read Korea's PC bang economics beyond a Wikipedia outline (100 to 25,000 bangs, 1997-2011, STALE; Nexon and Blizzard perks for logging in at a bang) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_bang). Riot's Philippine programme is known only from a sibling's search result. A publisher-funded perk would be a strong hook, but I found none in India.
- Bookings as a share of cafe revenue from birthdays or corporate events: nothing found. Dave & Buster's IR refused fetching.
- BGMI rank data access: no developer docs found. FACEIT's developer site refused fetching.
- Cross-cafe pass willingness among Indian gamers: no data.

#### Google rules that shape the design
- **Doorway abuse** includes pages aimed at specific regions or cities that funnel users on, and **scaled content abuse** includes transforming feeds into many pages without added value. - **LocalBusiness:** use the most specific subtype; address and name required; `additionalType` not supported — [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business). `InternetCafe` is a LocalBusiness subtype in schema.org — [schema.org](https://schema.org/InternetCafe). - **Stars:** a site can show LocalBusiness review stars only if it captures reviews about other businesses. No gaming equivalent is mentioned.

- **The simple route:** any business can add booking links to its Business Profile, up to 10 per category, and mark one preferred. These links cannot be set by API or spreadsheet, and performance data is not available for custom links — [links](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6218037), [providers](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7475773).

#### App links, deferred links and web versus app
- Android App Links are verified links to your own site that open the app without a chooser, on Android 6+ with Google services. Android 15 adds Dynamic App Links, which change URL rules without a new app release — [Android](https://developer.android.com/training/app-links). iOS Universal Links use a two-way site-and-app association, and Apple warns to validate parameters — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/supporting-universal-links-in-your-app).
- Expo Router enables deep linking for all screens automatically, and both link types fall back to the website when the app is not installed. Expo Go has limited support, so test on development builds — [Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/linking/overview/).
- Firebase Dynamic Links shut down on 25 Aug 2025. Google names Adjust, Airbridge, AppsFlyer, Bitly, Branch, Kochava and Singular as options, unvetted, for deferred links — [Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/support/dynamic-links-faq). On Android, the Play Install Referrer API returns the referrer URL after install — [Android](https://developer.android.com/google/play/installreferrer).
- App Clips can open from QR or NFC. The binary limit is 15 MB on iOS 16. iOS 17 allows 100 MB only for digital invocation, or for physical invocation through Apple's generated demo link — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appclip/choosing-the-right-functionality-for-your-app-clip).
- Web conversion precedent: BookMyShow's PWA lifted conversions over 80% and was about 440 KB, 54 times smaller than its Android app, with no date given (about 2017, STALE) — [web.dev](https://web.dev/bookmyshow/).
- Web-first booking exists in this niche: GameBiller's finder asks only for a mobile number — [GameBiller](https://www.gamebiller.com/book); ggLeap's portal embeds by iframe — [ggLeap](https://www.ggcircuit.com/ggleap); Intencity's form goes to WhatsApp and confirms in minutes — [Intencity](https://intencity.in). UPI is the default rail: 84.6% of Indian players have used it, against 31.5% for credit cards — [Niko, 11 Aug 2026](https://substack.nikopartners.com/p/indias-gaming-market-surpasses-1).
- WhatsApp costs: per-message pricing since 1 Jul 2025. Service messages are chargeable beyond 1,000 free a month from 1 Oct 2026. A click-to-WhatsApp ad opens a free window of up to 7 days. Indian accounts must move to INR billing by 31 Dec 2026 — [Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing). India's actual rates are in a linked card I did not read.

### Inferences
- **Build web booking, not just a promo site.** The app is where retention lives, but venue pages should book without an install, because the competition (phone, WhatsApp, Google) needs no app. The site then acquires and the app retains. BookMyShow's old case and District's checkout support this. It does not prove it for a low-income student audience.
- **Page matrix: real inventory only.** Start with city, locality and format pages (`ps5-gaming-cafe-in-{locality}`), and noindex anything under about 3 live venues. District's empty pages are a warning about Google's doorway language, and its thinness is your opening, because each of your pages can show specs, live slots, prices by tier and first-party reviews.
- **Venue pages should be the SEO asset.** Playo's traffic on venue names suggests cafe-name queries are worth owning. A sibling notes that Google Maps files many cafes under store categories, which may give well-structured pages an edge (my reading).
- **Markup choice.** Use `InternetCafe`, since Google allows an array of types if more apply, plus real first-party ratings. Event for tournaments only. FAQ markup adds little for Google.
- **Google route.** Have the field team set each cafe's Business Profile booking link on signing, since no API exists. Apply to Appointments Redirect after the first dense cluster, and ask Google directly whether gaming cafes qualify. Don't plan on it.
- **Deep links.** A single Next.js URL per venue, serving the page or opening the app, is enough at launch. Add a vendor for deferred links only when paid acquisition starts, and use Play Install Referrer first, since most users are likely on Android (not verified here).
- **AI agents:** cheap to prepare by allowing search-agent crawlers and publishing clean structured data, but referrals are negligible today. An MCP server can wait.

## 5. What would a differentiated brand and community look like to Indian gamers?

### Takeaway
The category's brands are converging on tournaments and rewards, while the one that has scaled abroad sells LAN culture and playing beside friends. The evidence supports a brand about squad nights and trusted floors more than about discounts or pro esports. Direct evidence on what Indian cafe-goers want from a brand is absent. Treat this as a hypothesis to test with cafe customers.

### Inferences
- **Suggested position, my judgement: the place your squad plays.** Hash and Console are chasing tournaments and earning. District owns generic going-out. Few own the Friday-night group ritual. LANGAME's playing-beside message shows it can scale. District can't easily imitate it, because of its ticket format.
- **Two signature rituals to test:** a weekly city LAN night, and a cafe-versus-cafe league where the cafe is the team. The second turns supply into a community partner, and a venue's own regulars into promoters.
- **Pro orgs are a distribution rental, not a brand.** S8UL-level deals look out of budget, and sponsorship is shrinking. Local micro-creators and campus captains are cheaper and more testable, though there is no evidence they bring bookings. City-based ISGL teams might suit watch parties, which is speculation.
- **Trust is a cheap differentiator.** Documents on file, verified specs, arrival guarantee. No rival appears to claim it.
- **Women and first-timers may be an open segment,** given the gap between overall female gamer share and the likely male-heavy cafe floor. I have no data on the floor itself, so test this with a few women-friendly session formats first.
- **The GTA VI launch cannot wait for an app.** It is 16 days away. Use it as a concierge test of bookings through WhatsApp and a landing page, mindful of Sony's commercial-use terms and avoiding game art.

### Gaps
- No survey of Indian cafe-goers on brand or why they choose a venue. A short floor survey would beat anything published.
- No evidence on creators driving offline visits, in any market. Red Bull Campus Clutch and Mood Indigo pages returned no text, so college-fest evidence is limited. No evidence found, not evidence of absence.
- Women's share of cafe visitors, and any safety complaints from women at cafes: nothing found.
- Discord usage among Indian cafe-goers: nothing found. Instagram could not be read.
- Red Bull, AFK Gaming, Talkesport, Skyesports and college fest coverage would be the next search targets.

### Inferences

**If I had to choose the first ten, in order (my judgement):**
1. A7 and F8: test demand by hand now, in one city, around GTA VI.
2. A4 and A5: pick a city without a strong rival and sign clusters. On sibling data, Pune has the best-known structure, and Mumbai has the most rivals.
3. C1 and D1: new-customer-only pricing with 0% on repeat, from day one.
4. E2 and E3: venue pages that book on the web.
5. E7: Google Business Profile booking link at signing.
6. B4: squad booking.
7. B9: arrival guarantee.
8. A8 and C4: free link, plus per-venue packs in the app.
9. A1, then A2: floor tool, then session control, in the launch cluster only.
10. C8 and F2: group desk and a cafe league, as the second revenue line and community hook.

**Things the evidence says to avoid or defer:**
- Commission on every booking at 15% or more. It invites the same grievance vendors already sell against.
- A stored cross-cafe wallet before a legal opinion.
- Paid-entry prize tournaments in the first release.
- A national page matrix before inventory exists.
- Stranger matchmaking as the launch hook.
- Reserve with Google as a launch dependency.

**A defensibility summary.** In this set, the durable assets look like live floor data, first-party reviews, player graphs and histories, and local density. Software features, discounts and SEO pages alone are easy to copy. District is the main threat because it has the demand, while its current ticket format has none of the floor data.

**Revenue-stack reality check.** Sibling's base case is about ₹31,500 a year per cafe. New-customer-only pricing lowers commission further, so this plan needs membership, group sales, sponsorship or hardware to reach venture scale, and that may not be achievable in India alone. A low-burn plan fits the Playo and FITPASS precedents better than a land grab. LANGAME's multi-language site suggests one competitor is already thinking beyond its home market, and the same option may apply here.

### Gaps
- Effort and defensibility ratings are judgement calls, and none has been checked with engineers or cafe owners.
- Nearly all gaming-specific performance figures are company or vendor claims. Interviews with 5 to 10 Pune cafe owners, and a floor survey of players, would test C1, B4, C4 and F1 faster than desk research.
- Search budget ran out before this work began. Planned but not run: practitioner writing on leakage and SaaS-enabled marketplaces, Indian press on District's gaming category, Russian press on LANGAME, Chinese and Southeast Asian cafe platforms (Meituan's cafe vouchers, Vietnamese and Indonesian cafe software), Red Bull and college-fest coverage, and India keyword data. A follow-up message can resume this, or the limit can be raised with `CLAUDE_CODE_MAX_WEB_SEARCHES_PER_SESSION`.
- Competition-law limits on price-parity clauses for venues were not researched, so no parity idea appears here.
